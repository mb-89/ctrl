# Research: analysis

Reader: whoever implements this part of Loop Tuner. Gathered by a research agent and checked by a skeptical reviewer on 2026-10-24; the reviewer's corrections are listed first and override the findings where they disagree.

## Reviewer's corrections

- Loop-break definition is wrong as written (Foundation step 6). With the loop closed (u_p = u_c + v), G(v→u_c) = −L/(1+L), so '−G(v→u_c)' gives the input complementary sensitivity T_i, not L. Checked numerically: with L = 3e^{−j} it returns 0.80−0.19j. Either open the loop (u_p = v) and take L = −G(v→u_c), or keep it closed and use L = −G(v→u_c)/G(v→u_p). That second form is the one the v2 multisine recommendation already uses (L = −U_c/U_p). S_i = G(v→u_p) is correct.
- The factor-sum phase ∠L = ∠K + Σ∠(e^{jωTs} − z_i) − Σ∠(e^{jωTs} − p_i), computed with atan2, is NOT continuous. In discrete time each factor winds around the origin, so it crosses the atan2 branch cut even for STABLE poles. Checked: poles 0.3±0.5j and 0.9 give a 360° jump at ω·Ts = 2.62 rad. RHP or outside-unit-circle factors jump too, so 'no unwrapping errors, including RHP poles' is false. A robust form: ∠(e^{jω} − p) = ω + ∠(1 − p·e^{−jω}) for |p| < 1 (that term's real part stays > 0), and ∠(−p) + ∠(1 − e^{jω}/p) for |p| > 1. Otherwise unwrap with adaptive refinement, anchored at the known low-frequency phase.
- Zeros through the matrix determinant lemma (det(zI−A+BC) − det(zI−A), then companion-matrix roots) are numerically unusable for ZOH models. Fast sampling bunches every pole near z = 1. Checked in Node: roots of a characteristic polynomial with s-poles −1…−n mapped by z = e^{sTs} have s-plane errors of 4.7 rad/s at n = 10, Ts = 10 ms; 16 rad/s at n = 15; and 3.5 rad/s already at n = 6, Ts = 1 ms. The library's encoder-motor example runs at Ts = 1 ms (plants.js). 'Fine for n up to about 15' is wrong. Use a structured state-space zero algorithm instead: Emami-Naeini & Van Dooren 1982 deflation followed by eig, or QZ on the Rosenbrock pencil. Note that the factor-sum phase depends on these zeros too.
- Nichols constant-|S| contour formula is wrong. |S| = Ms means |1+L| = 1/Ms, so L = −1 + e^{jψ}/Ms. The text's 'L = Ms·e^{−jψ} − 1' gives |S| = 1/Ms.
- Lissajous readout: Y1/R1 is the reference-to-output response G_ry, not T. Loop Tuner's PID has setpoint weight b, derivative on the measurement and a prefilter Tr (the tank uses Tr = 0.1), so G_ry ≠ T and the error amplitude ≠ R·|S(jω0)|. 'Mark T(jω0) on the Nyquist plot' mixes closed-loop and open-loop data. A measured point belongs on the L plot only as L = G_ry/(1 − G_ry), and only for 1-DOF unity feedback; otherwise use injection at u.
- 'PV–OP plot' is not another name for the person's plot. In the stiction literature (Choudhury, Shah & Thornhill) it is the controller output OP (= u) against the process variable PV (= y): plant input against plant output, not reference against output or error. Present it as a related XY channel choice (u vs y), not as the name.
- Shape catalogue overclaim: the clean shapes (backlash parallelogram, dead-zone flat step, clipped ends) are input–output characteristics of the nonlinear element itself. Inside a closed loop with integral action, r–y and r–e show these effects reshaped, typically as hunting or limit cycles and slow creep, not as the textbook figure. Say so, or suggest plotting u against y (or actuator command against actuator output) for diagnosis.
- 'ITAE subject to Ms ≤ 1.4 is a classic formulation': the classic Ms-constrained formulation is MIGO/AMIGO (Åström, Panagopoulos & Hägglund 1998; Åström & Hägglund 2004). It maximises the integral gain k_i, which minimises IE for a load step, subject to Ms ≤ 1.4. It does not minimise ITAE.
- The rule of thumb 'ωc·Ts ≈ 0.05–0.15 for discretised continuous designs' has no source and disagrees with the commonly quoted rule h·ωc ≈ 0.15–0.5 (a phase loss of about 4–14°), usually attributed to Åström & Wittenmark. Cite it or drop it.
- Trim by Newton on f(x,u) = 0 is underdetermined (n equations, n+m unknowns) and its Jacobian is singular in this library. Cart position and motor angle are free (a continuum of equilibria), and the tank needs u solved for a fixed level. A findop-style specification is needed: which states or inputs are fixed and which are free, solved by least squares or Levenberg–Marquardt with a pseudo-inverse. Newton started from x(t*) will not work as stated.
- The residual check ‖f(x*,u*)‖ will wrongly label steady motion as 'not an equilibrium': a speed-controlled motor at constant ω (θ̇ = ω ≠ 0, as in the encoder sweep example) or a cart moving at constant velocity. Exclude cyclic or ignorable coordinates, or test for a relative equilibrium (derivatives of the states that feed back ≈ 0).
- 'Run metrics follow MATLAB stepinfo defaults' does not describe the existing metrics.js. It normalises by the setpoint step (r1 − r0), judges settling against the setpoint r1 rather than yfinal, and computes the steady-state error as the mean over the last 5 % of the step window. With a P-only gain error, stepinfo reports a finite settling time; metrics.js reports Infinity. Choose one definition and label it in the panel.
- The performance budget is overclaimed for the root locus. A JS elmhes + hqr eigenvalue solve measured 0.065 ms at n = 15 and 0.135 ms at n = 20 (0.4 ms at n = 30). A root locus with 1000 adaptive gain points therefore costs about 65–135 ms, and the 200-value parameter locus about 13–27 ms, not 'a few ms'. The '<20 ms per edit' budget holds for margins, Bode and Nyquist only. Compute the locus progressively or in a Web Worker (a Blob-URL worker works in a single-file HTML).
- Delay margin DM = min PM_i/ωc_i fails for open-loop-unstable loops, where PM_i can be negative while the loop is stable. That would give a negative DM. Use DM_i = ((∠L(jωc_i) + 180°) mod 360°)/ωc_i, which is how allmargin treats it.
- 'A dead time becomes exactly d delay states (z^−d)' is true only for a delay at the ZOH input or at a sampled output. The diagram allows Dead-time blocks anywhere (diagram.js delay nodes; delay in tf blocks; delay in the sensor slot). A delay between continuous blocks or inside a plant-internal loop cannot be written as z^−d. Also, the 'continuous RHS f' is not an ODE when delay blocks are present. The linearizer must cut delay blocks out first, then reinsert them exactly or with a Padé/internal-delay model, and warn.
- Phase portrait for the swing-ups: this library measures θ = 0 upright (controllers.js wrap()). Wrapping θ to (−π, π] puts the hanging start (θ = ±π) and all early small swings on the wrap seam, so the trace jumps back and forth. Centre the window on the bottom, e.g. (0, 2π], for swing-ups, or let the user choose the wrap centre.
- Source problems: arXiv 2202.00498 is a thesis on LPTV/Floquet analysis. Its abstract does not mention frozen-time eigenvalues or the Markus–Yamabe/Vinograd examples; cite Khalil's Nonlinear Systems (the Markus–Yamabe time-varying example) or Rugh's Linear System Theory instead. arXiv 2304.07576 is about decentralized LQR; for the (1/2, ∞) and 60° margins cite Safonov & Athans 1977 or Anderson & Moore. All Åström & Murray section, theorem and example numbers are from the 1st edition (v2.10c, the PDF cited), not the 2nd edition (2021), where frequency-domain analysis is Chapter 10. Theorem 9.2, the §9.3 values and Example 9.8 (gm = 266, 70°, sm = 0.27, ζ = 0.014) are verified against v2.10c.
- 'Observer at 1 ms, controller at 10 ms' is not in the repository; the observer example is still pending. What does exist is a controller at Ts = 1 ms (the encoder motor). There the z-plane pole-zero view is useless because everything sits at z ≈ 1. Default to the s-plane view (s = ln z/Ts) and keep the z-plane as a toggle.
- Moving the XY plot onto the scope as a display mode is a design decision that departs from the person's words: they said analysis goes in its own tab and listed the r-vs-delta plot among the analysis diagrams. Record it as a decision with its reason, and make it reachable from the Analysis tab too.

## Summary

Control engineers judge a loop by three things. First, where the closed-loop poles are: the pole-zero map (PN-Bild) with a damping/natural-frequency grid, and the root locus (Wurzelortskurve). Second, how far the open loop L stays from the critical point −1: the Nyquist plot (Ortskurve), the Bode plot with gain margin (GM), phase margin (PM) and delay margin, and above all the peak sensitivity Ms = max|S| (the stability margin is 1/Ms). Third, time-domain numbers from the actual run: rise time, overshoot, settling time, IAE/ITAE and control effort. In Loop Tuner every model-based view should come from one linear model. Build it by linearising the plant numerically at a time picked on the scope, discretising it with a zero-order hold (ZOH) at the controller's sample time Ts, and breaking the loop at the single actuator input u. That gives one scalar L(z) for every controller variant, including 8-state LQR, and it matches the sampled simulation. A first version should have: a panel of key numbers (Kennwerte); a pole-zero map with root locus; a Bode plot with margins that can switch to the gang of four; a Nyquist plot with the Ms circle and a readout of open-loop unstable poles, encirclements and resulting closed-loop unstable poles; and an XY mode on the main scope. The plot the person described is an X–Y plot, called a Lissajous figure (Lissajous-Figur); when nonlinearities are present it is an input–output hysteresis loop (in process control, the PV–OP plot). The same XY mode with two states on its axes is a phase portrait. A linear lag gives an ellipse with sin φ = (y at x = 0)/y_max and area π·R²·|T|·sin φ; backlash, saturation, dead zone and friction each give a distinct shape. The Nichols chart, disk margins, frequency response estimated from simulation, a root locus over any controller parameter, and analysis of time-varying swing-ups are a second step. All of it runs in milliseconds in plain JS: a 1000-point frequency response of a 12-state model took about 5 ms in Node.

## Recommendations

### 1. Foundation: one linear closed-loop model per analysis, linearised at a time picked on the scope, discretised with ZOH, loop broken at u

**What.** An analysis engine that builds one discrete linear model of the whole loop. Inputs: r, d, measurement noise n, and an injection v at the plant input. Outputs: y, e, u at the plant (u_p), and u from the controller (u_c). Every diagram reads an entry of this model: L, S, T, PS, CS, r→y, r→u. The operating point is a draggable LIN marker on the scope's time axis, like a trigger marker. It defaults to the end of the last run.

**How.** 1) Operating point: x* = x(t*), u* = held controller output at t*, d* = d(t*). Report the normalised residual ‖f(x*,u*)‖. If it is not small (more than about 1e-3 relative to the state scales), label the analysis 'not an equilibrium: frozen-time view'. An optional Trim button runs Newton on f(x,u) = 0 starting from x(t*) and reuses the Jacobian; the pendulums trim to the upright position with u* = 0. 2) Plant Jacobians by central differences on the continuous right-hand side f, not on the RK4 step: A[:,j] = (f(x*+h_j e_j, u*) − f(x*−h_j e_j, u*))/(2h_j) with h_j ≈ 6e-6·max(|x*_j|, scale_j), since ε^(1/3) ≈ 6e-6. Build B (∂f/∂u), Bd (∂f/∂d), C and D the same way. That is 2(n+m) evaluations. 3) Controllers: make controller state explicit, step(state, inputs) → [state', u], instead of closures. Then any variant, including the gain-scheduled PI, can be linearised by finite differences on its discrete map (Ac, Bc, Cc, Dc), with inputs [r, y1..yk] and output u. Alternatively, give each variant a linear(params, Ts, op) method. The PID in controllers.js has states I, D, y_prev and the prefilter state. An actuator saturation counts as gain 1 if u is inside its limits at t* and 0 if it is saturated; warn in the second case. 4) ZOH discretisation by the Van Loan block exponential: expm([[A, B], [0, 0]]·Ts) = [[Φ, Γ], [0, I]], with expm by scaling and squaring plus a Padé approximant (Moler & Van Loan 2003). A dead time τ = d·Ts becomes exactly d delay states (z^−d). A fractional remainder needs the modified z-transform, which splits Γ into Γ0 and Γ1; otherwise round it and warn. 5) Multi-rate loops (observer at 1 ms, controller at 10 ms): lift by composing the N fast steps into one slow-rate map, which is exact and time-invariant at the slow rate. Until that is built, warn. 6) Loop break: inject v at the plant input, u_p = u_c + v, and define L(z) = −G(v→u_c). Then S_i = G(v→u_p) = 1/(1+L). 7) Eigenvalues: balancing, Householder reduction to Hessenberg form, then Francis double-shift QR (EISPACK hqr, or a port of JAMA's EigenvalueDecomposition; about 250 lines). Frequency response: reduce to Hessenberg form once, then do one complex solve per frequency (Laub 1981). A dense solve was measured at about 5 ms for 1000 frequencies at n = 12, and about 30 ms at n = 24. 8) Zeros of a single-input single-output (SISO) L with D = 0: by the matrix determinant lemma the numerator is det(zI − A + BC) − det(zI − A). Take its roots from companion-matrix eigenvalues; this is fine for n up to about 15. 9) With no controller yet, analyse the plant alone (poles and zeros, Bode of r→y), in line with 'what is not set up does not exist'.

**Why.** Root locus, Nyquist, Bode, the pole-zero map and the gang of four are all views of the same linear model, so compute it once. Computing it exactly as the simulation samples (ZOH at Ts) avoids the classic error of continuous analysis, which overstates phase margin by ωc·Ts/2. Breaking the loop at the single actuator gives a scalar L even for full state feedback, so every SISO tool applies. This is the same workflow as Simulink Control Design, which linearises at simulation snapshot times with linearize(mdl, io, t) and findop.

### 2. Key numbers panel (Kennwerte) with traffic-light thresholds

**What.** A compact table, the first thing the eye reads. From the linear model: GM, PM, crossover frequency ωc, delay margin, Ms, Mt, bandwidth, the closed-loop poles with damping ζ and natural frequency ωn, the smallest damping ζ_min, and a stability verdict. From the run (metrics.js already has these): rise, overshoot, settling, peak time, steady-state error, IAE, ISE, ITAE, u peak, u total variation, % saturated. Validity flags on top: saturated, not an equilibrium, nonlinear variant.

**How.** Report every −180° crossing as a gain interval (for example 'gain ×0.42 … ×3.1', also in dB), not one number. Report every 0 dB crossing and take the smallest PM. Delay margin DM = min over crossings i of PM_i[rad]/ωc_i, shown in ms and in samples (DM/Ts). Ms = max|S| and the frequency where it occurs; stability margin sm = 1/Ms; Mt = max|T|. Bandwidth ωB is the first frequency where |S| crosses 1/√2 from below (Skogestad). Pole data as MATLAB damp computes it: s = ln(z)/Ts, ωn = |s|, ζ = −cos∠s, τ = 1/(ζωn). Thresholds: PM 30–60°, GM 2–5 (6–14 dB), sm 0.5–0.8, that is Ms 1.25–2 (Åström & Murray §9.3); Ms below about 2 (6 dB) and Mt below about 1.25 (2 dB) (Skogestad §2.4.3). Ms also guarantees GM ≥ Ms/(Ms−1) and PM ≥ 2·arcsin(1/(2Ms)): Ms = 2 gives GM ≥ 2 and PM ≥ 29°; Ms = 1.4 gives GM ≥ 3.5 and PM ≥ 41.8°. Colours: Ms ≤ 1.4 green, up to 2 amber, above 2 red; ζ_min < 0.2 red. Run metrics follow MATLAB stepinfo defaults: rise time 10→90 %, settling band 2 % of |yfinal − yinit|. Show linear predictions next to the measured values for the dominant pole pair: ts ≈ 4/(ζωn) for the 2 % band (Franklin et al. use 4.6/(ζωn) for 1 %), and overshoot Mp = exp(−πζ/√(1−ζ²)).

**Why.** Engineers read margins first, and Ms catches what GM and PM miss. Åström & Murray's Example 9.8 has GM = 266 and PM = 70°, yet sm = 0.27 and a mode with ζ = 0.014. These numbers also become the auto-tune objective and constraints that come next; ITAE subject to Ms ≤ 1.4 is a classic formulation.

### 3. Pole-zero map with root locus (PN-Bild + Wurzelortskurve), damping/natural-frequency grid, gain marker you can drag

**What.** One plot: open-loop poles (×) and zeros (○) of L, the current closed-loop poles (filled squares), and the root-locus branches as a gain factor k on L runs from 0 to ∞. The current gain k = 1 is marked, and so are the gains where branches cross the stability boundary; these equal the GM limits in the numbers panel. A toggle switches between the equivalent s-plane and the z-plane.

**How.** Branches are eig(A_cl(k)) of the loop closed around k·L. Take k log-spaced over [1e-3, 1e3] relative to the nominal gain, plus the endpoints. Refine adaptively: bisect a k-interval whenever any pole moves more than 2 % of the view span. Match poles from one step to the next by nearest-neighbour assignment (greedy, or Hungarian for n ≤ 25). In the s-plane view, map s = ln(z)/Ts. Pin z = 0 (delay or deadbeat poles) at the left edge. Draw the band edges |Im s| = π/Ts, where negative real z poles land. Grid as MATLAB sgrid draws it: ζ = 0.1 … 0.9 as rays at angle arccos ζ from the negative real axis, and ωn circles in 1-2-5 steps. The z-plane view shows the unit circle and zgrid curves z = exp(Ts·ωn·(−ζ ± j√(1−ζ²))). Dragging the k marker scales the active variant's gains (P/I/D, or the K vector) and is undoable, like a knob. In v2, the same machinery gives a root locus over any one controller parameter (Kp, Ki, Tf, an LQR weight): re-linearise the controller for about 200 values of the parameter, a few ms in total.

**Why.** The person asked for it first. It shows directly how gain trades speed for damping and where instability starts. In the discrete form, dead time is exact (d poles at z = 0), so the tank's 0.1 s pipe delay appears without a Padé approximation, which would add fake right-half-plane zeros.

### 4. Bode plot of L with margins, switchable to the gang of four (S, T, PS, CS)

**What.** |L| in dB and ∠L in degrees over log ω. Annotate ωc, ω180, GM, PM and the delay margin. Draw a vertical line at the Nyquist frequency π/Ts. A toggle overlays the magnitudes of the gang of four with Ms and Mt marked. With a prefilter F, add TF and CFS (Åström & Murray's gang of six).

**How.** Grid: 600–1000 log-spaced points from ω_min = 0.1·min(smallest nonzero pole or zero magnitude, 2π/T_run) up to π/Ts. Add points near lightly damped poles, at ω = |Im s_i| ± ζ_i·|s_i|. Evaluate L(e^{jωTs}) on the discrete model. Compute the phase as a sum of factor angles, ∠L = ∠K + Σ∠(e^{jωTs} − z_i) − Σ∠(e^{jωTs} − p_i), so that it is continuous with no unwrapping errors, including when the open loop has right-half-plane poles. Find crossings by interpolation in log ω, then bisect on the exact L. Report all crossings, as MATLAB allmargin does. The gang of four come from closed-loop model entries: S = G(v→u_p) at the input; T = 1 − S; PS = G(d→y); CS = G(n→u).

**Why.** Bode is the everyday loop-shaping view. The gang of four exposes pole-zero cancellations that r→y hides: the tank's PI designed by pole cancellation looks perfect from r but reacts slowly to d. It also shows noise amplification into the actuator through |CS|, which matters for an unfiltered D-term and for the encoder example.

### 5. Nyquist plot (Ortskurve des Frequenzgangs) with −1, the Ms circle, and a readout of open-loop unstable poles, encirclements and closed-loop unstable poles

**What.** L(e^{jωTs}) for ω from −π/Ts to π/Ts: positive frequencies solid, the mirror image dashed. Show the −1 point; the circle centred at −1 with radius 1/Ms, which is the actual closest approach; a dashed target circle of radius 0.5 (Ms = 2); and the unit circle, where PM is read. Readout, for example: 'P = 3 unstable open-loop poles → needs 3 counter-clockwise encirclements; N = −3 → Z = N + P = 0 closed-loop unstable poles ✓'. Hovering a point shares the frequency cursor with the Bode plot.

**How.** Compress the radius logarithmically so that integrators and large gains fit: ρ' = ρ for ρ ≤ 1 and ρ' = 1 + ln ρ for ρ > 1. This mapping is monotone, keeps −1 in place and preserves encirclements. Draw rings at |L| = 1, 10, 100. Count encirclements as the winding number of 1 + L: accumulate arg(1 + L) along the contour, refining wherever a step changes the angle by more than 10°. Then N_cw = −Δarg/(2π); round it, and warn if it is off an integer by more than 0.05 (python-control's encirclement_threshold). Poles on the unit circle (integrators at z = 1) are handled by evaluating on |z| = 1 + δ with δ ≈ 1e-6, an outward indentation that counts them as stable. Draw the closing arc dotted. P is the number of open-loop eigenvalues with |z| > 1, including controller dynamics. Always check Z against the closed-loop eigenvalues. If they disagree, show 'count unreliable' rather than a stability verdict.

**Why.** Every pendulum in the library is open-loop unstable: P = 1 for the cart–pole, P = 3 for the triple pendulum. For such loops the Bode margins are ambiguous: the gain margin becomes an interval, and lowering the gain also destabilises. Only Nyquist's general criterion (Åström & Murray, Theorem 9.2: Z = N + P) gives a clean picture. Distance to −1 is the robustness measure. The person knows this plot as the Ortskurve.

### 6. XY mode on the scope: the Lissajous figure (r vs y, r vs e) and the phase portrait

**What.** A display mode next to YT, FFT and spectrogram. Channel 1 is the X axis; channels 2–4 are Y traces. Earlier runs fade as they do now. The person's plot is an X–Y plot or Lissajous figure (Lissajous-Figur). When nonlinearities are present it is an input–output hysteresis loop; process control calls it the PV–OP plot (stiction detection, Choudhury, Shah & Thornhill 2004). With two states on the axes (θ vs θ̇, e vs ė) the same mode is the phase portrait (Phasenporträt, phase plane). Real oscilloscopes have an XY mode, so it belongs on the display, not in a tab.

**How.** Readouts when X is nearly sinusoidal at ω0 (from the signal generator's Period): over the last whole periods after the transient, compute the first harmonics Y1 = (2/N)·Σ y·e^{−jω0 t} and R1 the same way. Then T(jω0) = Y1/R1 gives the gain |T| and the lag φ, and THD = √(Σ_{k≥2}|Y_k|²)/|Y_1|. Geometry, checked numerically: the ellipse fits the box ±R by ±|T|·R. sin φ = (|y| at r = 0)/y_max, which is the Lissajous rule sin φ = x0/a = y0/b. Area = π·R²·|T|·sin φ, the same in the r–e plot, because e = r − y is a shear. An output lag runs counter-clockwise in r–y and clockwise in r–e. The regression slope of e on r is 1 − |T|·cos φ. The error amplitude is R·|S(jω0)| for unity feedback. Overlay the ellipse the linear model predicts; any difference is nonlinearity. Mark T(jω0) as a point on the closed-loop Bode and Nyquist plots. Shape catalogue: a line of slope 1 is perfect tracking. A line of other slope is a gain error (P-only control gives T(0) < 1). An offset is a bias or load. An ellipse is linear lag, and it collapses as ω → 0. Clipped flat ends mean output saturation. Straight sloped flanks or a lens shape mean a rate limit: a saturated actuator turns y into a triangle wave. A parallelogram with flat top and bottom, 2× the gap wide, that stays as ω → 0 is backlash (rate-independent hysteresis; Nordin & Gutman 2002). A flat step through the origin is a dead zone. Corners and jumps at reversals are Coulomb friction or stiction (quadrant glitches). A figure-eight or a curve that does not close means harmonics, a limit cycle at another frequency, or a transient that has not settled. A test the tool can offer: repeat at a lower frequency. A dynamic-lag loop shrinks; a backlash or friction loop stays. For toggling (square) references, X takes only two values and r vs e degenerates into two vertical lines. There, offer the phase portrait e vs ė (or y vs ẏ): each toggle draws a path from (±Δ, 0) into the origin. A spiral means underdamped; its axis crossings are the overshoots; a straight segment at constant ė means rate or actuator saturation; a small closed orbit is a limit cycle. Alternatively, fold the trace so that each period overlays. For the pendulums, plot θ wrapped to (−π, π] against θ̇. Overlay the energy level of the upright position, the homoclinic orbit θ̇² = (2mgl/J)(1 − cos θ) with θ measured from the top, and the catch region |θ| < 0.4 rad. Together they show energy-based swing-up (Åström & Furuta 2000). Use state signals for derivatives where available; otherwise use smoothed central differences.

**Why.** This is the plot the person asked for. It needs no linearisation, so it works for nonlinear plants, swing-ups and the coming external (hardware-in-the-loop) plant. It also connects the time domain to the Ortskurve: each ellipse is one measured point T(jω0) of the closed-loop frequency response.

### 7. Layout and linking

**What.** The Analysis tab sits below the always-visible scope. A left column (about 240 px) holds the numbers panel. To its right, a 2×2 grid: [pole-zero map / root locus | Bode, two stacked axes, toggle L or gang of four] over [Nyquist (Nichols toggle in v2) | operating-point card plus linear-vs-simulated step overlay]. On narrow screens the grid becomes one column. The plots use the dark scope style.

**How.** Shared cursors: one frequency cursor across Bode, Nyquist and Nichols (and the gang of four). The gain marker in the root locus updates the numbers live and re-simulates, as the knobs do. The LIN time marker on the main scope sets the operating point; dragging it animates the poles and the Bode plot. For the conical tank this shows the plant pole moving with the level, which is the reason for gain scheduling. Debounce recomputation on every edit, with a budget under 20 ms. Show the linear model's step response as a reference trace on the scope, like a scope's REF waveform, to compare with the run. Put a banner at the top when the analysis is invalid: saturated x %, not an equilibrium, multi-rate, or a nonlinear variant active.

**Why.** This follows the instrument principles: the display stays visible, one tab is open at a time, gestures act as knobs. Linked cursors keep the diagrams consistent and teach how they relate: one ω is a point on the Ortskurve, a crossing on the Bode plot, and a gain on the root locus.

### 8. Treat sampling, the ZOH and dead time explicitly

**What.** Make discrete-time analysis the default and say so. Offer a continuous view only as an approximation, with the ZOH delay shown.

**How.** The ZOH acts like a delay of Ts/2 and costs about ωc·Ts/2 rad of phase margin: with Ts = 10 ms and ωc = 20 rad/s, that is 0.1 rad, or 5.7°. Holding the loss to 5° requires Ts ≤ 0.175/ωc. The simulation applies u at the sampling instant, which is idealised; a real controller loses up to one more Ts to computation. Offer a 1-sample delay toggle (one extra z^−1 state) and show its effect on PM and the delay margin. Rules of thumb for the readout: ωc·Ts ≈ 0.05–0.15 for discretised continuous designs; sample at least 20× the closed-loop bandwidth. Flag poles near z = −1, which ring at the Nyquist frequency (often from the D-term). Note that a discrete model cannot show ripple between samples.

**Why.** Every controller in Loop Tuner is digital with a ZOH. Continuous analysis would contradict what the scope shows, especially for the fast pendulum loops.

### 9. v2: Nichols chart with M-contours

**What.** Open-loop gain in dB against phase in degrees, with the critical point at (−180°, 0 dB). Constant-|T| contours (M-circles) and a constant-|S| (Ms) contour. It is a toggle in the Nyquist panel, using the same data.

**How.** For |T| = M: L = T/(1 − T) with T = M·e^{jψ}, sweeping ψ. Contours at +0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 and 6 dB, and at −1, −3, −6, −12 and −20 dB. Repeat them every 360° along the phase axis. MATLAB ngrid's default region is −40…40 dB by −360…0°. For |S| = Ms: L = Ms·e^{−jψ} − 1, which gives a closed exclusion contour around the critical point. Use the factor-sum phase from the Bode recommendation to avoid unwrapping jumps.

**Why.** Aerospace and QFT practitioners use it, and Mt can be read directly from it. It is less central in German curricula than the Ortskurve and the Bode plot, so it comes after them.

### 10. v2: disk margins and LQR guarantees

**What.** A single robustness number for simultaneous gain and phase variation, plus the guaranteed bound for the library's LQR designs.

**How.** Disk margin α = 1/‖S + (σ−1)/2‖∞. The balanced case σ = 0 gives α = 1/‖(S − T)/2‖∞. Disk gain margin [(2−α)/(2+α), (2+α)/(2−α)], disk phase margin ±2·atan(α/2) (Seiler, Packard & Gahinet 2020; MATLAB diskmargin: α = 0.458 gives 0.63…1.59 and ±25.8°). A continuous-time LQR satisfies |1 + L| ≥ 1 at the plant input, so Ms ≤ 1, GM ∈ [1/2, ∞) and PM ≥ 60°. The tool's discrete LQR (Ts = 10 ms) only satisfies |1 + L(e^{jω})| ≥ √(R/(R + ΓᵀPΓ)), derived from the discrete return-difference equality. Show the bound Ms ≤ √(1 + ΓᵀPΓ/R) next to the actual Ms. Observer-based state feedback (LQG) has no guaranteed margins (Doyle 1978); show the drop in margin when the observer example arrives.

**Why.** Margins from one loop at a time can miss gain and phase changing together. The LQR guarantee is a classic teaching point, and the discrete case shows how sampling erodes it.

### 11. v2: frequency response estimated from simulation (also for the future external plant)

**What.** A model-free L(jω) for variants or plants that cannot be linearised: energy swing-up, freeform diagrams, the external plant.

**How.** In a background copy of the simulation, inject a Schroeder-phased multisine v at the plant input, with amplitude about 2–5 % of the u range. Use frequencies at multiples of 1/T_record; a 5 s record gives 0.2 Hz resolution. Compute L(jω_k) = −U_c(ω_k)/U_p(ω_k) and S_i(jω_k) = U_p(ω_k)/V(ω_k) from DFTs over whole periods. Overlay the result on the model-based curves; where they differ, the model or linearity breaks down. For nonlinear loops this is a describing function and depends on the amplitude.

**Why.** Network analysers measure loop gain this way, by injection at a breaking point. It makes the Ortskurve available wherever the model is not, including hardware.

### 12. v2: time-varying and hybrid controllers (swing-up, trajectory + time-varying LQR, gain scheduling)

**What.** Honest analysis for variants that are not linear time-invariant.

**How.** Analyse the variant or mode that is active at t*; after the catch, that is the upright LQR. For trajectory + time-varying LQR, multiply out the linearised closed loop along the plan, Φ_cl(k,0) = Π(A_i − B_i·K_i), and plot its largest singular value over time to show how perturbations grow or shrink during the swing-up. Mark frozen-time eigenvalues as indicative only. For gain scheduling, linearise with the schedule frozen and note that this holds only while the scheduling variable changes slowly.

**Why.** Frozen-time eigenvalues with negative real parts do not guarantee stability of a time-varying system (the Markus–Yamabe and Vinograd counterexamples). The swing-ups are the library's showpieces and need their own view, not a misleading Bode plot.

## Pitfalls

- Continuous-time analysis of a sampled loop overstates phase margin by ωc·Ts/2 (the ZOH half-sample delay) and ignores any computation delay. Use the ZOH-discretised model; its frequency axis ends at π/Ts, and it cannot show ripple between samples.
- Dead time is exact in discrete time (d delay states, z^−d) and in the frequency response (e^{−jωτ}). A continuous root locus or pole-zero map needs a Padé approximation, which adds fake right-half-plane zeros. A delay that is not a whole number of samples needs the modified z-transform.
- Linearising at a point that is not an equilibrium (mid-swing, mid-transient) describes a drifting trajectory. Check and show ‖f(x*,u*)‖ and label the result as a frozen-time view. Finite differences across non-smooth points (Coulomb friction at v = 0, a saturation edge, a switch) average two slopes and mean little.
- Finite-difference steps: use h ≈ ε^(1/3)·scale (about 6e-6) for central differences. Too small a step gives round-off; too large a step picks up nonlinearity. Differentiate the continuous f, not the RK4 step map. For the controller, differentiate its discrete update.
- Linear margins assume the actuator is unsaturated and anti-windup is inactive. The swing-ups run at the ±25 N and ±40 N limits, so show the percentage of saturated samples and say that the linear analysis does not describe the run.
- Open-loop unstable plants (all the pendulums): Bode margins are ambiguous. GM is an interval, since lowering the gain also destabilises, and MATLAB's margin warns. Nyquist needs P counter-clockwise encirclements, and the phase starts at −180° for each right-half-plane real pole.
- Counting encirclements is numerically fragile near integrators, near poles on the unit circle, and on coarse frequency grids. Indent the contour (|z| = 1 + δ), refine adaptively, round with a tolerance of 0.05, and cross-check against the closed-loop eigenvalues. Never show a verdict that the eigenvalues contradict.
- The loop-breaking point matters. Margins at the plant input (the actuator) differ from margins at a sensor, and only the input break gives a scalar L for multi-sensor state feedback. One-loop-at-a-time margins also miss simultaneous variations; disk margins cover them.
- Pole-zero cancellations hide modes: r→y can look perfect while d→y is slow or an unstable mode is hidden. Always list all closed-loop eigenvalues and show the gang of four, not just T.
- Good GM and PM do not imply robustness. Åström & Murray's Example 9.8 has GM = 266 and PM = 70°, but sm = 0.27 and a mode with ζ = 0.014. Always show Ms and ζ_min.
- Some limits are physics, not tuning. A cart–pole controlled from the cart position alone has a right-half-plane zero at z = √(g/l) close to its unstable pole p, so Ms ≥ |z+p|/|z−p| (Skogestad eq. 5.73; Ms ≥ 42 for m/M = 0.1). Large peaks in sensitivity at that output are unavoidable.
- Discrete poles mapped to the s-plane with s = ln(z)/Ts: z = 0 maps to −∞, negative real z maps to ±jπ/Ts, and the log is multi-valued. Pin such poles at the edges and say so.
- Multi-rate loops (observer at 1 ms, controller at 10 ms) are periodically time-varying. Analyse them by lifting at the slow rate, not by mixing the two rates.
- Bode and Nichols phase-unwrapping errors produce false crossings. Compute the phase from the angles of the pole and zero factors, or start unwrapping from a known low-frequency phase.
- The Lissajous / XY plot needs a periodic steady state and a sine or triangle excitation. Drop the transient and fit over whole periods, or the harmonics leak. Square references make r vs e degenerate into two vertical lines. A lag runs counter-clockwise in r–y but clockwise in r–e.
- Step metrics depend on their definitions: rise time 10–90 %; settling band 2 % of |yfinal − yinit| in current MATLAB stepinfo, where older releases used the peak error; the final value is taken from the last sample. In a 5 s run that has not settled, settling time and steady-state error are wrong. Measure per reference event.
- Frequency response estimated from a 5 s simulation has 0.2 Hz resolution and says little about low frequencies such as integral action. For nonlinear loops it depends on the excitation amplitude.
