It covers two things the design rests on: Pugh's concept selection and how control people judge one controller against another. Gathered on 2026-10-25. Where a source was found only second-hand, it says so.

## 1. Pugh's concept selection

**What it is.** Stuart Pugh (Loughborough, then Strathclyde) described it in the 1980s and in *Total Design: Integrated Methods for Successful Product Engineering* (Addison-Wesley, 1991). It is a matrix: the concepts are the columns, the criteria are the rows. One concept is the **datum** (the reference, often the existing design or the best-known option). Every other concept is marked against the datum on each criterion: **+** (better), **S** (same) or **−** (worse). The method is deliberately not numeric: you only say better or worse than the datum. [Cambridge IfM, controlled convergence](https://www.ifm.eng.cam.ac.uk/research/dmg/tools-and-techniques/controlled-convergence/); [Wikipedia, Stuart Pugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Pugh); [MIT ESD.33 lecture notes on Pugh concept selection](https://mitocw.ups.edu.ec/courses/engineering-systems-division/esd-33-systems-engineering-summer-2004/lecture-notes/s7_pg_cnpt_slctn.pdf).

**The steps** (as the textbooks teach it; the book itself was found only second-hand):
1. Choose the criteria from the requirements. They must tell the concepts apart; a criterion where everything is S does no work.
2. Choose a datum. Pugh's advice is a strong one: the best concept you know.
3. Mark each concept + / S / − against the datum, criterion by criterion.
4. Count the +, S and − per concept. Read the counts as guidance, not as a score: Pugh warned that the marks "must not be summed algebraically" ([lecture deck quoting Pugh](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/pugh-matrix-concept-evaluation-in-design/49669603)). Weighted totals are a later, common variant (Σ weight × (+1, 0, −1)), used in Six Sigma and in Ulrich and Eppinger's concept screening.
5. Look at the strong concepts' − rows: can a concept be improved there, or can two be combined? Add the hybrids.
6. **Iterate**: run the matrix again with the strongest concept as the new datum. Concepts fall away, new ones come in, the set converges ("controlled convergence"). Pugh's point was that analysis and synthesis alternate.

**What is known about how well it works.** Frey, Herder, Wijnia, Subrahmanian, Katsikopoulos and Clausing simulated design teams using the method (*Research in Engineering Design* 20(1): 41–58, 2009, doi 10.1007/s00163-008-0056-z; [TU Delft record](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/the-pugh-controlled-convergence-method-model-based-evaluation-and), [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/49448)): a **strong datum helps convergence**, repeated matrices help the experts' opinions converge, and the method did better than one summary criterion or a Borda count under a wide range of assumptions. The criticism: Hazelrigg argued from Arrow's impossibility theorem that rank aggregation over several criteria can be inconsistent ("The implications of Arrow's impossibility theorem on approaches to optimal engineering design", ASME J. Mech. Des. 118: 161–164, 1996; his comment on Frey et al., *Res. Eng. Design* 21: 143–144, 2010). The practical answer, which the design takes: **show the numbers under the marks**, keep the marks relative to one datum, and treat the total as a guide for a person's decision, not a verdict.

**What this means for the benchmark.**
- The marks come from measured numbers, so "better" needs a tolerance: within it, S. Without one, a 0.1 % difference would be a +.
- The datum matters: a weak datum makes everything +. A badly tuned running variant (the oven's, J 1.28) is a weak datum; a PID tuned by the same procedure as the others is a strong one.
- Iterating (step 6) and the hybrids (step 5) are left to the person: the benchmark makes one matrix against one datum, the tuned PID (the person, input 48: "set a good datum and then the others are either plus or minus one … Don't make it too complicated"). Running it again after a change is the iteration.
- Hard requirements are not criteria: a concept that fails one is out, not "−". In controls: a loop that diverges, or is unstable where it runs.

## 2. How control benchmarks judge controllers

### Benchmark plants
- Åström and Hägglund, "Benchmark systems for PID control", IFAC *Digital Control: Past, Present and Future of PID Control*, 2000 ([Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/537934)): a set of transfer functions (lag-dominated, delay-dominated, integrating, oscillating, non-minimum-phase …) for testing PID rules. The lesson: judge a method on a spread of plants, not one.
- Wie and Bernstein, "Benchmark problems for robust control design", ACC 1990/1991 (doi 10.23919/ACC.1991.4791727, found second-hand): a two-mass–spring system with uncertain stiffness. Every design is judged on the same plant, the same disturbances and the **same uncertainty range**, and against settling time, effort and robustness at once. That is the shape the benchmark takes: one plant, one experiment, one variation range.

### The criteria in practice

| Criterion | Usual measure | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| Following the reference | IAE, ITAE, ISE of e = r − y after a setpoint step; overshoot; rise and settling time (2 % or 5 %) | ITAE punishes errors that last (Graham and Lathrop 1953). The repo's cost already has IAE/ITAE/ISE ([../output/autotune.md](../output/autotune.md)) |
| Rejecting a disturbance | IAE after a load step at the plant input | For process control the more important one: Åström–Hägglund's MIGO/AMIGO maximise integral gain (≈ minimise IAE for a load step) under a robustness bound; Garpinger and Hägglund minimise IAE for an input load step ([Garpinger, Hägglund, NPCW 2009](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/npcw09/A-S-Garpinger-O-1.pdf)) |
| Effort | IAE or rms of u, or of u about its mean; peak u | Skogestad's SIMC work and Grimholt–Skogestad weigh input usage alongside IAE (see [autotune.md research](autotune.md)) |
| Actuator wear | **Total variation** of u, Σ\|Δu\| | The standard measure of "how hard on the actuator" (Skogestad 2003 uses TV for input smoothness). It is what separates an ideal sliding mode from its boundary layer (§3 of the design) |
| Saturation | share of time at a limit; windup | A loop that lives at its limit is open; anti-windup decides how it comes back |
| Robustness margins | **Ms** = max\|S\| (≤ 1.4 good, ≤ 2 acceptable), gain margin (> 2), phase margin (> 30–45°), delay margin; Mt | Skogestad and Postlethwaite, *Multivariable Feedback Control*, ch. 2; Åström–Hägglund. Ms bounds both margins: GM ≥ Ms/(Ms − 1), PM ≥ 2 arcsin(1/(2 Ms)). The repo computes them all ([analysis.md research](analysis.md)) |
| Robustness to plant change | The criteria again on perturbed plants (a parameter grid or random draws), worst case and spread; Kharitonov or μ for linear sets | Scenario robustness is what works for nonlinear and switching laws, which have no margins. The person's HIL plant varies by ±15 % (input 10) |
| Noise sensitivity | Noise gain from measurement to u (high-frequency gain of C, \|C(e^{jπ})\| at Nyquist); SDU, the standard deviation of u from measurement noise | Romero Segovia, Hägglund and Åström propose SDU as the noise counterpart of IAE ([Lund](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/ed14e22b-d167-491b-a19a-b43a423c4e05)); Garpinger, Åström and Hägglund, "Performance and robustness trade-offs in PID control", *J. Process Control* 24(5): 568–577, 2014, frame load rejection, robustness and noise injection as one trade-off (found via [Åström's slides](https://canal.uned.es/uploads/material/Video/21276/Conferencia_Karl_Astrom.pdf)) |
| Implementation complexity | Number of parameters, measurements needed (full state vs output), whether a model is needed, nonlinear or switching law, sample rate needed | Usually a judgement; here derived from the registry |
| Tuning effort | Experiments or simulations needed; whether a model must be identified first | The repo's methods already report runs |

**The trade-off is the point.** Every source above says the same: load rejection, reference following, robustness, effort and noise pull against each other. A ranking on one number (the tuner's J) hides that. Iterate's J has no robustness term, so tuning to J alone can buy a little IAE with a lot of robustness: the prototype found exactly that (the tank's schedule tuned to Ms 12, phase margin 9°; [benchmark-prototype.md](benchmark-prototype.md)). MIGO's answer, optimise performance subject to Ms ≤ M, is the one the design adopts for tuning.

### Fairness: comparing tuned controllers
- Compare each structure **at its best on the same goal**, not as it happens to be set ([autotune.md research](autotune.md), the reviewer's note: Grimholt–Skogestad compare IAE-optimal controllers at a fixed Ms). Otherwise the benchmark measures the tuning, not the structure.
- The same experiment (reference, disturbance, duration, sampling), the same actuator limits, the same robustness bound, the same tuning budget.
- A structure that needs a measurement the plant does not provide (ẏ, the full state) is not worse: it does not apply, and the benchmark says what it would need.
- Some tasks are not "follow r" at all (a swing-up must bring the pole up); a shared cost cannot judge them (the autotune research reviewer's "per-variant goals").

## 3. Scales finer than + / S / − (input 55)

The person: "plus minus one is not enough … there needs to be probably a 5-point scale … research … if there's something better." The benchmark's criteria are measured numbers, so whatever scale is chosen can be set by rule, not by judgement (input 55: "it can't be judgment … just mechanical").

| Scale | What it is | For the benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Pugh's three marks, + / S / − | The original: better, same, worse than the datum; the marks are counted, not summed ([Wikipedia, Stuart Pugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Pugh)) | Too coarse: a candidate 7× worse at following and one 15 % worse both get one −. On "DC motor, deadbeat" Hu–Lin "won" (+3) with 7.3× the PID's following error, from many small + rows |
| **Five marks, −2 … +2** (++ much better, + better, S, − worse, −− much worse) | The common extension of Pugh's matrix in engineering-design teaching and Six Sigma tools ([t2informatik, Pugh matrix](https://t2informatik.de/en/?p=20757)); seven marks, −3 … +3, in some tools ([SigmaZone Quantum XL](https://help.sigmazone.com/quantum-xl/v18/statistical-tools/qfd-fmea-pugh/pugh/overview)) | Keeps the matrix readable at a glance, against a datum, and separates "a bit worse" from "much worse". Needs a rule for "much": below |
| QFD's 0-1-3-9 | Relationship strengths in a House of Quality; the nonlinear spacing makes strong relationships dominate ([Boeing QFD material](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/sys/ac/docs/bfrptpa4/tsld035.htm)); its normalisation can reverse ranks (Raharjo 2013, [Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/180245)) | Made for "how strongly does X affect Y", not "better or worse than a datum"; it has no sign. Its idea, that a large difference should count more than linearly, is what the −− mark gives |
| A utility per criterion, weighted sum (SAW, the weighted decision matrix) or TOPSIS | Each number mapped to [0, 1] (min–max or vector normalisation) and weighted; TOPSIS ranks by distance to the ideal and the anti-ideal (Hwang and Yoon 1981; [Bilkent course notes](https://courses.ie.bilkent.edu.tr/ie102/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2023/04/TOPSIS.pdf)) | Continuous and mechanical, but the result depends on the normalisation and on which candidates are in the set (adding a bad candidate rescales everyone; rank reversal), and a person cannot read it at a glance. No datum |

**Recommendation: five marks, set by the ratio to the datum.** For a criterion where lower is better, with a, b the candidate's and the datum's numbers and f the criterion's absolute floor, q = (a + f)/(b + f); the mark is S inside the criterion's tolerance (1/(1 + tol) < q < 1 + tol), + or − beyond it up to a factor 2, ++ or −− beyond a factor 2. Reasons: the ratio is what control engineers read ("half the error", "twice the noise"); a factor 2 is a round, plant-independent line for "much"; the floor keeps tiny numbers (0 % against 1 % overshoot) from reading as infinite ratios; the marks depend only on the candidate and the datum, so adding or removing candidates changes no other column (unlike min–max or TOPSIS). The continuous log-ratio (log₂ q, clipped at ±2) is the same idea without bands; it is kept as an option for the sensitivity analysis only, since bands are what the person asked for.

## 4. How robust is the winner to the weights (input 55)

The person: "assume our weights are wrong. How robust is a solution against change of weights? … how would the weights have to change for a close-up to win? And is that change reasonable?" With marks m and weights w, a candidate's total is T = Σ wᵢ mᵢ: linear in w, so the standard questions have exact answers.

- **One weight at a time** (Triantaphyllou and Sánchez, "A sensitivity analysis approach for some deterministic multi-criteria decision-making methods", *Decision Sciences* 28(1): 151–194, 1997, doi 10.1111/j.1540-5915.1997.tb01306.x; [record](https://iaorifors.com/paper/33359)): for the winner W and a runner-up j, the change Δ of weight i at which they tie is (T_W − T_j) / (m_ji − m_Wi). The smallest such |Δ| over i is j's threshold; the criterion it names is the one the decision hangs on.
- **All weights together**: the smallest change ‖Δw‖ for which j ties W is the distance from w to the hyperplane Σ wᵢ (m_ji − m_Wi) = 0 (projected to keep weights ≥ 0); reported relative to ‖w‖.
- **Random weights**: Butler, Jia and Dyer, "Simulation techniques for the sensitivity analysis of multi-criteria decision models", *EJOR* 103(3): 531–546, 1997 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v103y1997i3p531-546.html)), draw weights at random and count how often each alternative wins. SMAA (Lahdelma, Hokkanen and Salminen, *EJOR* 1998; [Aalto abstract](https://sal.aalto.fi/publications/abstracts/plah98.html); Lahdelma and Salminen in *Trends in Multiple Criteria Decision Analysis*, 2010, doi 10.1007/978-1-4419-5904-1_10) names the share of weight space where an alternative ranks first its **rank-1 acceptability index**, and the mean of those weights its **central weight vector**.

**"Reasonable", mechanically**: the default weights are on a scale of 1 to 3, so one step is 1. A change is reasonable when every weight stays within ±1 of its default and above 0. The SMAA draws are uniform within that box. **Robust** means no reasonable single-weight change makes a runner-up tie the winner, and the winner takes at least 75 % of the draws (interpretation); otherwise **sensitive**, naming the runner-up and the weight.

## 5. The disturbance test and feedforward (input 55)

- **A standard disturbance**: control practice judges load rejection with a load step at the process input. Åström and Hägglund's MIGO and AMIGO and Garpinger and Hägglund minimise IAE for a load step at the input (§2); Grimholt and Skogestad evaluate PI and PID by the IAE of an input disturbance and an output disturbance, at fixed robustness ([Grimholt, Skogestad, *J. Process Control* 2018](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/publications/2018/grimholt-skogestad-jpc-2018-Optimal-PI-and-PID-control-of-first-order-plus-delay-processes/grimholt-jpc-pid-2018.pdf)), and treat a setpoint change as a special case of an output disturbance. The input is the one place every plant has, and a load there passes through the whole plant, so only integral action (or something equivalent) removes it.
- **Feedforward and steps**: model-based feedforward is designed together with a smooth setpoint. Lambrechts, Boerlage and Steinbuch, "Trajectory planning and feedforward design for high performance motion systems" (ACC 2004; [paper](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/acc04/Papers/0827_FrM03.2.pdf)), plan fourth-order (jerk-limited) trajectories because the feedforward Kv·ṙ + Ka·r̈ (+ snap) needs the derivatives of r to exist and stay within the actuator; Boerlage, Tousain and Steinbuch add jerk-derivative feedforward ([TU/e](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/jerk-derivative-feedforward-control-for-motion-systems-2/)). On a raw step only the static part Kff·r acts (zero for an integrating plant such as the motor's angle); ṙ and r̈ are impulses, one sample long, clipped by the actuator. So a benchmark that only steps r cannot see what feedforward does; a smooth move of the same size can. The prototype measures it (the design, §2.5).

## 6. A generic framework: judged criteria, controlled convergence (input 57)

The person: "we are using PUG matrices in other places too. So if you find a better way of weighing options, that can be reused for other things … Not at a cost of the quality of what we're doing here." The benchmark's criteria are all measured; elsewhere many are judgement ("maintainability", "fit with the team").

**Relative judgement beats absolute rating.** Thurstone's law of comparative judgment (1927; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_comparative_judgment)) rests on the observation that people are inconsistent when they rate one thing on its own but consistent when they compare two ([Warwick teaching note](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/statistics/news/teachingforum/ian_jones_-_comparative_judgement_-_090322.pdf)); comparative judgement in education assessment builds on it (with the caveat that reliability needs many comparisons). Pugh's matrix is already comparative: every mark is "better or worse than the datum". So judged criteria are marked **against the datum on the same five-point scale**, not rated absolutely; a judged mark and a measured one mean the same thing.

**Weighted decision matrix versus Pugh's controlled convergence.** Ulrich and Eppinger's *Product Design and Development* separates **concept screening** (+ / 0 / − against a reference, Pugh's matrix) from **concept scoring** (ratings 1–5 against a reference, weighted and summed), the finer stage when concepts must be told apart ([MIT 15.783 slides](https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-783j-product-design-and-development-spring-2006/4a44d148fc00f946862177da626cb069_cls9_cncpt_sel_6.pdf); [SNU notes](https://ocw.snu.ac.kr/sites/default/files/NOTE/4750.pdf)). Pugh's own method is about iterating: improve the strong concepts where they are weak, combine them, run the matrix again (§1; Frey et al. 2009 found the repeated matrices made the experts' opinions converge). The framework takes from each what does not cost the measured case anything:
- the **weighted five-point marks against a datum** (scoring's resolution, screening's relative marks);
- **several raters per judged mark**: the median is the mark (robust to one outlier), the spread is reported, and the weight-sensitivity draws pick one rater per cell at random, so disagreement between people shows as uncertainty in the share of draws (SMAA treats uncertain criterion values the same way, §4). A measured criterion has one value and is untouched;
- **weak spots for a hybrid**, Pugh's step 5 made mechanical as a hint: for each row where the suggestion is worse than the datum, the fitting candidates that are better there. It changes no mark and no rank; iterating (adding a hybrid, running again) stays with the people deciding.

What was **not** taken: QFD's 1-3-9 (no sign, §3), absolute 1–5 ratings (less reliable than relative marks, and they would need a second rule beside the ratio bands), pairwise-comparison models such as AHP or Bradley–Terry for the judged marks (they need every pair compared, n(n−1)/2 judgements per criterion, too heavy for a matrix filled in a meeting).
