# Tab 3: the Controller

Reader: whoever changes controllers, their library or the Controller tab. **Status: built 2026-10-24** (decided with the person the same day; their words in [../input/2026-10-24-08-controllers-and-5-seconds.md](../input/2026-10-24-08-controllers-and-5-seconds.md)).

**The controller is a block in the plant diagram, and a block holds several interchangeable implementations (variants, as Simulink's variant subsystems).** All variants of a block have the same interface, so flipping between them changes nothing else; Auto-tune will search their parameters and flip through them.

## The Controller block

- Inputs: r first, then the measurements, as many as the block's **Inputs** (default 2: r and y). Output: u.
- Digital: every variant runs at the block's **sample time Ts** and holds u between samples (zero-order hold). Several Controller blocks may have different Ts. **A controller samples exactly at k·Ts, whatever the scope's record step** (`stepPlan` in `src/core/sim.js`; before, a 1 ms controller at a 2.5 ms record sampled at 0, 1.667, 2.5, 3.333 … ms). Either the integration step divides the greatest common divisor of the record step and every Ts (in whole nanoseconds), so every sample instant lies on the grid, or a step of the stability bound is cut at each sample instant inside it; whichever takes fewer steps (a Ts of 1.02 ms at a 2.5 ms record would need a grid of 125 steps per record; cutting takes about 4). The end of each step is read on the nanosecond grid, so a reference that steps at a sample instant reaches the controller at that sample.
- JSON: `{ "type": "controller", "params": { "inputs": 2, "Ts": 0.01 }, "variants": [ { "name": "PD", "type": "pid", "params": { … } } ], "variant": 0 }`. Open actuator limits are saved as `null`.

## The controller library (`src/core/controllers.js`)

| Type | Law | For |
|---|---|---|
| `off` | u = 0 | Seeing the plant on its own |
| `pid` | Kp(b·r − y) + Ki∫e − Kd·ẏ filtered (Tf), on the prefiltered rf (Tr, the Vorfilter); reference feedforward Kff, Kv, Ka; limits with switchable anti-windup (clamping, back-calculation, off); start output u0 | Most loops |
| `stateFeedback` | u = kr·r − K·y, one gain per measured state; kr is the static prefilter; optional Tr | Matrix controllers (Zustandsregler), LQR, pole placement |
| `gainScheduled` | PI with Kp, Ki interpolated from a table over the measurement; the PID's prefilter, feedforward and anti-windup | Plants whose dynamics change with the operating point |
| `swingUp` | Far from upright, u = kE·E·ω·cos θ (V = ½E² falls: a Lyapunov design) with cart centring; near upright, state feedback | Swinging a pendulum up and catching it |
| `relay` | u = u0 ± d by the sign of e, switching when e passes ±ε (hysteresis); a negative d reverses it | On/off control (a thermostat); Auto-tune's relay experiment |
| `slidingMode` | Inputs r, y, ẏ. s = λ·e + ė with e = rf − y, ė = ṙf − ẏ (ṙf = (r − rf)/Tr, the prefilter's own derivative; 0 without one); u = u0 + K·sign(s) (φ = 0, the relay) or u0 + K·sat(s/φ) (φ > 0, a boundary layer), clamped. λ in 1/s, K in u (negative for a plant whose y falls when u rises), φ in y's unit per second. A block with fewer than 3 inputs is refused ("sliding mode needs ẏ on input 3: set the Controller block's Inputs to 3 … (a Finite difference observer makes it from y)"; adding one to such a block in the Controller tab says so and adds nothing): the law never differentiates. Linear only with φ > 0: a PD, D = [[K·λ/φ, −K·λ/φ, −K/φ]] on [r, y, ẏ]; φ = 0 has no linear model | Robust position or speed loops on an actuator that is a switch; on a continuous one, the layer. On the line the error decays as e^(−λt) whatever the inertia or a load entering with u: λ sets the error's time constant. *DC motor, sliding mode* |
| `superTwisting` | Inputs r, y, ẏ; the same s. u = k1·√\|s\|·sign(s) + v, v += Ts·k2·sign(s) (explicit Euler), both clamped to the limits (v's clamp is the anti-windup); u0 starts v. No linear model (√\|s\| has infinite slope at 0) | A continuous voltage that rejects a constant load (v integrates sign(s)), in finite time; with actuator dynamics it keeps a small ripple, and a long reaching phase winds v up so s overshoots the line |

### Designed types: computed from the plant

Designed types hold inputs only (weights, levels, times, limits, and hand gains such as the PI's feedforward) and compute their numbers in the page from the plant the block sees, every time the diagram changes; nothing plant-specific is stored ([designs.md](designs.md); the person, [input 09](../input/2026-10-24-09-analysis-autotune-triple-swingup.md)). The plant a block sees is the map from its output u to its measurements, from the same compiled diagram that runs, with other Controllers holding their output.

| Type | Inputs | Design method | Copy as numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lqr` | Q (per measurement), R, track, Tr, limits | Discrete LQR (zero-order hold at Ts) on the plant linearised at the origin, which must be at rest with every state measured: K = Kx C⁻¹ in measurement order; kr gives the tracked measurement a static gain of 1 from r | `stateFeedback` |
| `scheduledPI` | levels `at` of the first measurement, Tcl, Tr, the reference feedforward Kff, Kv, Ka, limits, anti-windup | At each level: trim (the u that holds it), linearise, fit a first-order lag K e^(−θs)/(τs + 1) on the path from u; pole cancellation Kp = τ/(K·Tcl), Ki = 1/(K·Tcl). u0 holds the level the plant starts at. The dead time θ is reported and left out. The feedforward and the anti-windup are passed on as they are | `pid` (one level) or `gainScheduled` |
| `pidFF` | the PID's Kp, Ki, Kd, Tf, b, Tr, u0, limits, anti-windup; order (which feedforward terms) | The plant's inverse from u to the first measurement at its origin, 1/G ≈ h0 + h1 s + h2 s², plus what the PID's P (b ≠ 1) and D take away: Kff = h0 + Kp (1 − b), Kv = h1 + Kd, Ka = h2 − Kd·Tf (designs-in-the-page.md §15) | `pid` |
| `swingUpLqr` | the energy pump's hand gains, Q, R, limits | The catch by `lqr` at the upright rest, and the pole's length and g the pump measures energy with; the pump's gains stay by hand | `swingUp` |
| `trajectory` | T, segments, planShare (the Plan fold), start, Q, R, limits | A force per sample that takes the plant from its start state to rest at the origin (multiple shooting, Levenberg–Marquardt, angles modulo 2π), within a share of each limit, time-varying LQR along it, the LQR at the origin after it, checked on the nonlinear plant as it runs. Made in the background in the page (a few seconds, with progress and Cancel); a limit or the plan's own inputs plan again | none: a plan is always computed |

The **Observer block** (inputs u, y1, y2, …; outputs the estimates x̂1, x̂2, …; its own Ts) holds variants from `src/core/observers.js` the same way: `kalman` (designed: the model of the states the Observer sees and the steady-state gain L by `dlqe`; R from the noise and quantizer blocks on each measurement when left empty; Copy as numbers gives `luenberger`), `kalmanModel` (a Kalman filter on a model you type in, A, B, C with Q and R: the page computes L from them, for a plant it cannot see, such as an External plant; a file's `kalman` with a typed-in model opens as it), `luenberger` (A, B, C and L by hand) and `difference` (finite differences). A Controller designed on a Kalman filter's estimates looks through them to the states they stand for, so LQR on x̂ gives the K it gives on the states.

Every controller type also has `linear(p, Ts, at, values)`: its discrete state space from [r, y…] to u about an operating point, for the Analysis tab (designs.md, "For the Analysis tab"); a type that has none there (a relay, the ideal sliding mode, super-twisting, a swing-up pumping energy) throws, and the tab shows why.

The diagram of what the running variant does (`src/core/structure.js`) draws a designed type as its Copy as numbers twin, with the numbers its design computed (`–` until they are), and a line under it saying where they come from; the planned swing-up has a picture of its own (the plan's F(t) and y*(t), TV-LQR on the error, the LQR after T).

To add one: an entry in `CONTROLLERS` with `label`, `summary`, `params`, `make(params, Ts)` returning `{ step(inputs) → u }` and, when the law is linear about an operating point, `linear` (a designed type adds the fields in designs.md); a sentence in `ABOUT` in `src/ui/controller.js`; a picture in `LAYOUTS` in `src/core/structure.js`; a test. A `check(params, ctx)` may refuse a variant; `ctx.inputs` is the block's input count when the variant sits in a block (the sliding-mode laws refuse fewer than 3). Its tag in the System library: `VARIANT_TAGS` in `src/core/systems.js`; Auto-tune's knobs: `tuningSpace` in `src/core/autotune.js` (for the sliding modes: λ, K and φ when φ > 0, so a relay stays a relay; λ, k1 and k2; a designed type is tuned as its Copy as numbers twin, from the numbers its design computes).

The sliding-mode gains in the library are design inputs (λ, φ, the limits) and hand gains (K, k1, k2: K equals the limit; k1 and k2 started from Levant's rule of thumb k1 = 1.5√L/β, k2 = 1.1L/β with a guessed L = 1000 rad/s³ at the nominal β, 1.9 and 44; then k1 raised to 4 by simulation, since 1.9 overshoots the step by 17.8 % with a tripled inertia; 6 would also remove the line overshoot there but costs more ripple); no formula is stored, none runs in the page. k2 = 44 itself is the rule's number from the motor's β, so it is derived from the plant: an open question for the person (roadmap; the library review, v2.7.4) ([sliding-mode.md](../research/sliding-mode.md)). The library's guard (design.test.mjs) allows them beside the designed types, `pid` and `off`: they are tuning inputs, not numbers derived from the plant. A model-based law (equivalent control) and the implicit super-twisting law need plant values, so they would come as designed twins.

## The tab

Four columns: the block (which one, if several; Inputs, Ts; what feeds each input), the **variants** (click one to run it, double-click to rename, Duplicate, Delete; a designed one is marked "computed", or "! no design" when its design was refused), the running variant's **parameters** (typed with SI prefixes, vectors comma-separated and their entries named; the wheel nudges a number by 5 %, Shift 1 %, or the entry under the caret in a focused list, and the display follows at once) with, for a designed variant, the read-only **Computed** box and **Copy as numbers**, and the **library** in two groups, From the plant and By hand (adds a variant that keeps the actuator limits and gets one gain or weight per measurement).

Comparing: the run before a change stays faded on the display for five runs, so flipping a variant or turning a parameter shows old against new.

**What it does**: under the variant list, a small block diagram of the running variant, drawn from its type with its live values on the blocks; it follows every parameter change. A path whose gain is 0, or a part that is off (no prefilter, no limits), is drawn faint. Hovering a block says what it is; a click shows the diagram large (Esc closes it). Per type: the PID's reference path (prefilter Tr, setpoint weight b), the P, I (∫, with the start output u0 as a second line) and D paths (d/dt, on the measurement, filtered; its minus sign is the one at the summing bar), the feedforward, the summing bar, the limits and u, and its anti-windup as set (clamping: a dashed line from the limits to the integral; back-calculation: the (u − v) loop through a 1/Tt block, with Tt in the note under it; off: a warning on that row); the scheduled PI with its table over y (the table's line to the gains dotted, the anti-windup's dashed; back-calculation's Tt as the range over the schedule's points); state feedback with kr and one row K1 … Km per measurement, named by what feeds it; the swing-up's pump and catch with the switch between them and its condition; the relay's hysteresis; off; and for Observer blocks predict and correct (Kalman, Luenberger) or the finite difference. Times read in ms below 0.1 s, numbers below 0.001 in µ; a note longer than the canvas is cut with … and whole in its tooltip. The description is a pure module (`src/core/structure.js`, tested in Node: every type has a picture inside its canvas, with its values, and nothing is drawn over a note or through a block, in every anti-windup mode); `src/ui/ctldiagram.js` draws it as inline SVG in the theme's colours (class `cdg`). A type without its own layout gets one block with its summary. The variant list keeps all its rows: the Variants column (up to 320 px wide, so the diagram draws at about 1.15 times its size on a desktop) scrolls when the diagram needs the room. Interpretation: the person asked for "a small diagram of what the controller does in the corner that's still open"; that corner is the empty space under the variant list, and a fixed layout per type reads better at that size than an automatic one.

## The standard controller: reference path, feedforward, anti-windup

The person: "nobody that does serious control theory does it without feed forward. So our controllers need to support it", and anti-windup "needs to be in", switchable so one badly tuned example can turn it off ([input](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md)). The PID and the gain-scheduled PI have both; every new parameter defaults to what ran before (feedforward 0, clamping), so existing projects behave the same.

**What industrial controllers offer** (checked briefly): process PIDs take a feedforward value that is summed into the output after the PID terms, inside the output limits (Rockwell's PIDE has an FF input of that kind, and WindupHIn/LIn to stop the output at a limit). Servo drives and motion controllers add, in the position loop, a velocity feedforward (the commanded profile's speed times a gain) and an acceleration feedforward (its acceleration times a gain, an inertia estimate), computed from the command, not from the error; they cut the following error without raising the loop gains and do not change stability (Machine Design, "20 minute tune-up: Forward march"; Power Motion, "Feedforward compensates for servo-loop errors"). Disturbance feedforward (from a measured disturbance) is out of scope here.

The law, at each sample, on the prefiltered reference rf and the measurement y (`pidCore` in `src/core/controllers.js`):

| Part | Term | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Prefilter **Tr** | rf: r through a first-order lag Tr (0: rf = r) | Smooths reference jumps before the controller sees them (the Vorfilter). It changes what is asked, not the loop |
| Setpoint weight **b** | P = Kp·(b·rf − y) | How much of r the P part sees (two degrees of freedom): b < 1 softens the answer to a step of r without changing how the loop rejects disturbances |
| I | Ki·∫(rf − y), from u0 | Removes what is left |
| D | −Kd·dy/dt through a lag Tf | Damps; on the measurement, so a step of r gives no kick |
| Static feedforward **Kff** | + Kff·rf | The input that holds y at r, per unit of r: for a plant with a steady gain K, Kff = 1/K |
| Velocity feedforward **Kv** | + Kv·drf/dt | What following a moving reference takes: for the motor, the voltage per rad/s of speed |
| Acceleration feedforward **Ka** | + Ka·d²rf/dt² | What accelerating takes: inertia |
| Limits, anti-windup | v = P + I + D + FF; u = v held to [umin, umax] | See below |

- The derivatives of rf are differences over Ts. Before the first sample rf is taken to have rested at its first value, so the first sample puts out no kick; a reference that then starts moving does kick (it really accelerates).
- **A first-order prefilter makes a step smaller for them, not smooth**: the Kv term jumps to about Kv·Δr/Tr (instead of Kv·Δr/Ts) and falls with Tr, and the Ka term still spikes, Ka·Δr(1 − a)/Ts² and then −Ka·Δr(1 − a)²/Ts², a = e^(−Ts/Tr): 57 V and then −5.4 V for a 2 rad step with Tr 0.1 s and Ka 0.03 (a test). On step references Ka therefore hurts (the square-wave motor's PD: IAE 1.17 → 1.32); Kv helps there only with a prefilter and an integral (*PID with prefilter* + Kv 2.2: IAE 1.68 → 1.12, settling 1.25 → 0.38 s). Kv and Ka are for references that are smooth themselves (a sine, a ramp, a planned profile).
- **Why b is not feedforward**: b scales the reference inside the P term, so what it adds, Kp(b − 1)·r, is tied to the feedback gain and is used to soften the answer to r. Feedforward supplies, from r alone, the input the plant needs to follow r (its inverse), independent of the feedback gains. With b = 1 and no feedforward the controller only acts on the error, so it can act only after an error has appeared; feedforward acts as r changes.
- **Feedforward and the parts on y**: when y follows r exactly, the D part still brakes (−Kd·ṙ) and P still puts out Kp(b − 1)·r. A feedforward that removes the following error therefore adds them back: Kv = the plant's need + Kd, Kff = the plant's need + Kp(1 − b). In *Following a sine (XY)* the motor needs 0.7 V per rad/s and Kd is 1: Kv = 1.7, set by hand, lays the figure flat (σ about 21 times smaller than the PID's once the start-up has died away; slope −0.0003 instead of −0.028).
- **Anti-windup** (`aw`), judged on the total output v, feedforward included:
  - **clamping** (conditional integration; the default, as before): while u sits at a limit, the integral stops if the error would push u further.
  - **back-calculation**: dI/dt = Ki·e + (u − v)/Tt, so the integral is pulled back toward the limit with time constant Tt (per sample I moves by the fraction 1 − e^(−Ts/Tt) of the excess, exact for a held excess). **Tt** 0 takes the rule of thumb of Åström and Hägglund, Tt = √(Ti·Td) (between Td and Ti), and Ti/2 for a PI (Td = 0), with Ti = Kp/Ki and Td = Kd/Kp; never below Ts; the scheduled PI takes Ti/2 at the gains of its operating point. Interpretation: Ti/2 for a PI is our choice inside the range the rule asks for. After the error reverses, the output leaves the limit Tt·ln 2 later (a test). **The feedforward's own excess beyond a limit is left out of (u − v)**: the tracking error is that of v with the feedforward held to the limits. A kick of Kv or Ka as r starts to move saturates u for a few samples; the integral cannot undo it, and tracking it left I offset once the kick had passed (−0.5 V to −4 V with y following r exactly; *PID with prefilter* + Kv 2.2 on the square-wave motor stalled 5 % short of r and never settled within 2 %). Left out, the same loop settles in 0.41 s (IAE 1.13, clamping 1.12) and the oven with back-calculation is unchanged (peak 52.5 °C). Interpretation: the review's fix, the smallest that keeps back-calculation's behaviour for everything the feedback asks.
  - **off**: the integral runs on (windup). *Oven, badly tuned* has the same gains with it off: the heater stays full on past r and the first step overshoots to 67 °C instead of 52 °C (69 % instead of 29 %).
  - Without a Ki there is no integral and nothing to wind up. Clamping now compares the direction of Ki·e (not e) with the limit, so a reverse-acting PID (negative gains) clamps the right way.
- **Edits while running** (Live mode changes the parameters in place, [hil.md](hil.md)): every parameter is read at every sample; the scheduled PI reads the feedforward, anti-windup and limits each sample as the PID does. A change of Kff, Kv or Ka is **bumpless**: at the sample of the change the integral takes the step the new gains would put into u, and lets it go at its own pace (only with a Ki; without one nothing could carry it, so u jumps). On the sine, switching Kv from 0 to 1.7 at 2.5 s kept u at −0.9 V instead of jumping to −3.0 V; the largest error in the second after fell from 0.072 to 0.013 rad. Interpretation: bumpless parameter change is what industrial controllers do; we do it for the feedforward, which this branch adds. Kp, b and the other gains change as before.
- **The linear model** (`linear(p, Ts, at)`, for the Analysis tab): the feedforward is in r's column, with the states it needs (r one and two samples back, only when Kv or Ka is not 0); the prefilter stays outside the matrices, and the model carries its Tr so the tab's step goes through it as the law's does ([analysis.md](analysis.md)). Anti-windup acts only at a limit, where the model does not hold, so it is not in the model. `test/analysis.test.mjs` checks the model against `step()` sample by sample, feedforward included, and the step through the prefilter against the law in a loop.
- **Auto-tune** keeps a tuned PID's feedforward and anti-windup; relay and step results move a feedforward that is on with their new gains (Kv by the change of Kd, Kff by the change of Kp(1 − b)), so it keeps doing its job. Iterate tunes the feedforward first with the feedback held ([autotune.md](autotune.md)).
- **Follow-up**: a feedforward gain that is really the plant's inverse (Kff = 1/K, Kv from the plant's speed gain) should be computed from the plant the block sees by the design layer ([designs-in-the-page.md](designs-in-the-page.md), follow-up). Until then the examples' feedforward gains are hand-set tuning inputs.
