The design said 0: the library's tank, at u = 0, drains to empty during Listen and sits below its reference at the band's edge; its PI is set to start in balance at 0.172 m³/s, which holds it at 0.15 m. Interpretation: the person's own start value says where the plant rests.
- **Listen reads its last half** for the mean, the noise and the drift (the step q from all of it): a plant still settling from before (the tank emptying, an arm still swinging) would read as noise and drift. Drift counts only when the slope stands 4 standard errors out of the noise; y₀ is the level at Listen's end, carried along the drift. - **A rung's reach is measured from y₀; its response from where it started.** The move rule and the next rung's bound use the reach (the room actually used): a plant still swinging from the rung before starts the next one away from rest, and its excursion read from that moving level was wrong both ways. Visible, plateau, slope, ringing and p are read against the level the rung started from.
- **A rung waits for rest.** After its planned rest (2T, a pulse 3T) it stays at u₀ until y stops moving (less than 5 % of its peak over half a window, or 6 floors/√w), at most 8 × its length (at least 1 s) and never past the Nudge's 40 %. Without it, a lag or an integrator whose dynamics outlast 2T started every rung from a moving level and the first look read a lag with the wrong sign. An unstable plant never comes to rest; still growing at the end, beyond 1.5 × the reach where the push would have peaked, it runs away.
- **The growth exponent p is clamped to [1, 2] for a doublet and is 1 for a pulse** (the design: [1, 3]). A doublet's spectrum 4a·sin²(ωT/2)/ω at most quadruples when T doubles, a pulse's 2a·|sin(ωT/2)|/ω at most doubles, for every linear plant and whatever its dead time; p = 3 only made a plant with a dead time longer than the push (the push shows nothing, so p took its largest value) look 8× riskier, and the Nudge stayed at 20 ms pushes. Unreadable, p takes its largest physical value.
- **Doubling a push that has not visibly moved y predicts × 3, from at least 5 floors.** Friction lies at tiny amplitudes (the research): on the arm, 0.48 → 0.96 V moved it 2.9–3.5 × further (Coulomb's 0.01 N·m is 0.2 V), so the design's × 2 (with its 1.5 safety) failed its own bound on 8 of 50 arms; a reach under 5 floors is the noise's, not the response's. Doubling T and doubling a visible push keep the design's 2^p and 2. With these, every rung of 50 arms stays within the bound predicted before it ran (worst 0.98 of it).
- **A pulse's two sides are predicted and held to their own rooms**, a doublet's swing both ways to the smaller room as designed: a heater at 0 % only pushes y up.
- **The response's sign is the direction of its first clear move**, not y(T): with a dead time longer than the push, y(T) is noise.
- **Nothing visible within the room ends the test with a sentence**: "angle moves too little to read within half of 'may move within' (its noise is …): widen it", or, at the budget, "… too short to see angle move clearly". Measured: the tank at its start with 2.2 mm of noise ends so (half its room below is 0.11 m, the visibility bar 44 mm); at 1.5 mm it completes.

"Rings" is a resonance with ζ < 0.5 or two swings in the rest; for the Hold a resonance counts as a lag of its gain and of 1/(ζωn) (it rings) or its slower time constant. After the rest, still moving away from y₀ beyond 1.5 × the largest reach: it runs away.
- **The arm rings only without its friction.** At the Nudge's ±0.2 rad, Coulomb friction stops the arm within half a swing (it takes 4·Tc/mgl ≈ 0.4 rad a cycle): the first look sees an overdamped second order (ζ 0.9–1.3 at 0.5–0.75 Hz against the linear 1 Hz, 0.22), the research's "tiny amplitudes lie". ## 2026-10-25 · Identify, being built: the kernel, the multisine and the frequency response (§18 step 3)

Built on branch `feat/identify` from [identification.md](identification.md) §5.6, §6.7 and §6.8 (no new words from the person): `tcKernel`, `kernelStep`, `kernelGuess`, `tcInverse`, `multisineLines`, `multisine`, `frf` and `wholePeriods` in `src/core/fit.js`, and `eigSym` in linalg.js. Where the build made the design more precise, or measured it wrong:

- **The kernel's search: one eigendecomposition per λ, not one Cholesky.** The design searches λ alone (a grid, then golden section, at most 40 evaluations), so c and σ² must be found at each λ. With K = U E Uᵀ (the tridiagonal inverse's factor) one symmetric eigendecomposition of E^½ Uᵀ ΦᵀΦ U E^½ gives −2 log L for every c in O(n), σ² profiled exactly; a Cholesky gives it for one (c, λ) only. It costs about four Choleskys (10 ms at 150 taps); about 22 evaluations; the estimate takes one Cholesky. Checked against the direct N×N formulas to 1e-9.
- **Decimation keeps y at its full rate**: a tap sums the impulse response over D lags, its regressor is u's mean over them, and every regressor is strictly past, so with white noise the kernel is consistent under feedback. Interpretation of "block-averaged and decimated": averaging y too would add a lag-0 term the loop biases.
- **An integrating plant is fitted as a rate plus a decaying TC part, not on Δy** (the design's "fitted on Δy"). Measured under a PI: on Δy, whose noise is coloured and fed back, k′ came out 3.45 for a true 3 on every seed; as a rate, within 1.3σ.
- **The research's example reproduced**: 2e^(−0.1s)/((0.5s + 1)(0.1s + 1)), 30 s, SNR 3 as a ratio of standard deviations: impulse-response fit 94.3–96.1 % (mean 95.1 %) at 150 taps of 20 ms, against the research's 95.6 % at 300 taps (300 taps here: 94.4–95.3 %). As a ratio of variances the fit is 91.4–94.2 %; the research's numbers match the first reading.
- **The multisine's clip-and-restore clips at 1.4 × the rms** (a share of the peak converged more slowly in 20 passes): crest factor 1.7–2.0 for 18–34 lines, 2.3–2.7 for 60 lines over 765 harmonics. Schroeder's phases are offered beside the design's random ones; on these sparse, log-spaced lines they start no better.
- **The distortion share**: per line, the noise removed in power, odd and even detection lines each against the response at their neighbouring excited lines, the two added in power. Pooling all detection lines would let a cubic's empty even lines halve its share. Measured with a cubic actuator: odd only about its centre, quartered by half the push, odd and even away from it; in open loop the odd detection lines' level is 0.87 × what lands on the excited lines (30 phase realisations).
- **Ĝ's bars**: from the spread between periods with the U–Y covariance term, as designed; under a brisk PI they hold the truth at 91.9 % of lines (four periods predict 92 %); without the covariance they would be 1.4× too wide near crossover. Where the loop's noise spectrum is steep (under integral action, near a lightly damped resonance) the spread overstates the variance up to 3×: too wide, not too narrow.
- **A period with a `seq` gap at or inside it is dropped** (`wholePeriods`), also a gap just before its first sample (the plant's steady state is broken). The period after a gap still carries a small transient (1e-5 of Ĝ on a lag); the experiment (step 8) may want to drop it too.

## 2026-10-25 · Identify, being built: the numerics (§18 step 2)

Built on branch `feat/identify` from [identification.md](identification.md) (no new words from the person): `cholesky`, `cholSolve` (linalg.js) and `src/core/fit.js`. Where the build made the design more precise, or measured it wrong:

- **θ is fitted in samples, bounded below by 0, not in logs.** The design put positive parameters in logs; θ = 0 is a common answer that logs cannot reach. Gains keep their start's sign; τ, f, ζ and k′ are fitted in logs.
- **The bars follow the loop on closed-loop segments.** σ̂²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ assumes white residuals; the replay's residual is the measurement noise filtered by the loop (S·v), lifted where the loop rings and removed where its integral acts. Measured on a resonance under a brisk PI over 40 seeds: f covered by its ±2σ in 31 of 40, K's bar 1.6× too wide. The covariance now uses the replayed loop's noise transfer S, found by one extra replay with a small error at the segment's start (cov = A⁻¹ σv² (SᵀJ)ᵀ(SᵀJ) A⁻¹, A = JᵀJ); every parameter is then covered in 36–40 of 40. On open-loop segments it is σ̂²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ exactly, as designed. Still assumed: the measurement noise is white (the floor); with coloured noise the bars understate (the design already says the bars understate when the structure is wrong).
- **A segment starts from the settled mean, not one sample.** Under a gentle loop, one noisy start sample is an offset the replay carries for seconds: it moved K by 9σ in one seed. The segment builder (step 5) passes the mean over the settle as `yStart`.
- **"What is left looks like noise"**: at most one of 25 lags outside ±2.58/√N and none beyond 1.5 × the band (white residuals fail this in about 3 % of records; "all 25 inside" would fail 22 %). The autocorrelation removes no mean (Ljung), so an offset is a pattern; the cross-correlation with past u removes u's mean (its level is arbitrary). Both stay hints, never verdicts.
- **Choosing**: the fewest parameters within 1 point of the best Check fit, then BIC; **a BIC difference below 2 decides nothing**, and the structure listed first (lag, two lags, resonance, integrator · lag, integrator · resonance) wins. A resonance with ζ ≥ 1 is exactly two lags, so on such a plant the two fit alike and BIC alone would choose by rounding.
- **Levenberg–Marquardt**: forward-difference Jacobians (p + 1 replays per iteration, as costed), Marquardt scaling, Nielsen's damping, bounds by projection; it yields after each iteration so the page can slice it by work, never by time. Measured: every structure recovers its parameters from noise-free data to 1e-6 (open and closed loop; the design asks 1 %), against a fine-step simulation that shares no code with the fit.
- **Coloured noise in closed loop** (AR(1), a = 0.9 at 20 ms): fitting the model to the measured u puts the noise the controller fed back into the plant and finds the dead time 70 % long; the replay of the law is unbiased (10 seeds, within 2.5 standard errors).

## 2026-10-24 · v2.7: time control, after review

Two reviews of the first build (no new words from the person). What changed, and the interpretations it took:

- **∞ runs at the simulation's own speed, and no setting runs faster.** The page times the screen's simulation after each change (screen seconds over the wall time it took; the fastest of up to three timings, the later two only while the speed is held to it, as the first is slowed by the browser compiling). Found in review: ∞ swept a screen per frame, so its figure was the screen's length times the frame rate (0.55× on a 10 ms screen, 646× on a 50 s one), not a limit of the system. Interpretation: the sweep replays one simulation, but it is paced as if it were simulating, so the readout tells how fast the page can run the system ("show how fast the time is actually running"). Consequence, said plainly: the simulation takes 2000 steps for any screen, so the limit depends on the time per division, and a screen the page simulates slower than real time (the oven at 1 ms/div: 0.7×) runs below 1× at every setting. **Ask the person** (roadmap.md, open questions); this revises "As fast as possible completes each sweep in a frame" below.
- **A finite speed keeps time on short screens**: a frame that ends a sweep goes on into the next, round the screen as often as it takes, and all of it counts (it was capped at one sweep a frame: 1× ran at 0.59× on a 10 ms screen). Found in review.
- **Auto-tune's runs last their own length** (one period of the input) at the speed, not the screen's sweep: a 5 s run takes 5 s at 1× on a 1 s or a 10 s screen (it ran at 4.5× and 0.67×). **The next run is computed while one sweeps** and starts in the frame the one before ends, so 10× reads 10× (it was 8×). At ∞ a slice of computing stops before a step would overrun it, an improvement is its own task at most four times a second, and the tuner's run stands for the screen's when they are the same simulation. **The display stops for every method**, so at ∞ the readout counts the tuner's computing (relay and step showed the screen's replay). **The scope's Stop stops the tuning** while a run sweeps (it skipped to the next run). A run on the display pauses while the tab is hidden, as the sweep does. Found in review; the pacing details are ours.
- **At ∞ only a changed run fades the earlier ones**, so after an edit, or at the end of Auto-tune at ∞, the run before stays to compare (it was gone within five frames). At a finite speed every sweep still fades them, as the person set (five runs); whether high speeds should count only changed runs too is open.
- **The control**: six digits and a field wide enough for them; a number too large to hold (`1e400`) is refused, not taken for ∞; Esc goes back to the speed from before the field took the focus, the arrows' steps included; one header menu open at a time (Speed ▾, System library ▾, Save ▾). **Held at 1× by a live plant**: read-only in the secondary ink (not faded), a lock at the field's left, and a click or tap says why in a toast, so touch users see the reason too; the readout keeps a fixed width, so nothing moves on Connect. The readout's tooltip gives the simulation's speed; "caps out" is no longer in the page's text.
- **The autosave is written when the page is closed or reloaded**, not only 300 ms after a change.
- For agents, `loopTuner.speed()` also returns `simulation`, the simulation's speed.
