## 2026-10-25 · v2.9: when the Check cannot tell candidates apart, another check ([input 56](../input/2026-10-25-56-dead-time-vs-resonance-another-check.md))

- What happened on the arm: it is strongly nonlinear (gravity with cos θ, friction; its gain changes 3–4× over its range), so every linear candidate misses the Check by 17–21 % (the noise would allow 99.6 %). Those misses swamp what tells a dead time from a resonance, which lies in the first ~150 ms after each push and in a small ringing; one fit % over the whole Check is blind to it, and §6.4's rule ("fewest parameters within 1 point") took the lag with 0.15 s of dead time for an arm that moves within 2 ms.
- The person: "They are two different things ... if you run into that situation, there should be another check." Decided, all mechanical:
  - **Onset test** (data already recorded): after each push, the time y first leaves its noise band; a candidate whose dead time is longer than that is refuted (✕ "moves before its dead time"). On the arm this removes the 0.15 s dead time at once.
  - **A discriminating test** when two candidates still tie: from the two fitted models, the page picks the test input within the safe band (a pulse, a step, a burst at either model's characteristic frequency) on which their predictions differ most against the noise floor, runs it, and keeps the model that predicts it (model-discrimination design: Box and Hill 1967; Atkinson and Fedorov's T-optimality, 1975).
  - The Shake's frequency response (step 8) shows the same directly: a resonance has a peak in magnitude and its phase drop near f; a dead time has a flat magnitude and a phase falling in proportion to f.
- To be built in identification.md's steps by the Identify session, before the Shake.

## 2026-10-25 · v2.9: the benchmark weighs disturbances, judges feedforward on a smooth move, marks on five points, gates the main job, tests its weights ([input 55](../input/2026-10-25-55-benchmark-scale-disturbance-sensitivity.md))

The person, after the prototype ranked Hu–Lin first on "DC motor, deadbeat" with 7.3× the PID's following error: "it can't only weigh following, it also needs to weigh disturbance reaction … feed forward doesn't gain anything on steps. That's very hard for me to believe … plus minus one is not enough … probably a 5-point scale … how would the weights have to change for a close-up to win? Design only ([benchmark.md](benchmark.md) §2.5–§2.9, §13; research [../research/benchmark.md](../research/benchmark.md) §3–§5; the prototype re-run on the three systems and the deadbeat motor):
- **Disturbance rejection always counts.** When no input but r plays anything, a standard load step at the plant's input, on a copy: −10 % of the actuator range (of u's swing when the range is open), from the middle of the longest stretch between r's steps to the end. **Interpretations**: the plant's input because every plant has one and a load there needs integral action (the input load step of Åström–Hägglund and Grimholt–Skogestad); 10 % so the same share is left to answer on every plant; a system's own d, when it plays, stays as it is.
- **Feedforward on a smooth move.** A second following test: r's steps eased by 1/(τs + 1)³, 99 % done in a fifth of the shortest stretch between steps. Measured: the feedforward supplies 75 % of the effort on the position motor's move against 16 % on its raw steps, and halves the error (0.061 against the PID's 0.078). On a raw step only Kff·r acts (0 for an integrating plant); Kv·ṙ, Ka·r̈ are one-sample kicks the limits clip. **Interpretations**: following's weight 3 split 1.5 steps + 1.5 move, so following and rejection keep the person's 3 and 3; the cost a candidate is tuned to is the sum over both tests; a third-order lag stands in for a jerk-limited trajectory.
- **Five-point marks by rule**: q = (a + f)/(b + f) against the datum; S within the criterion's tolerance, ±1 up to ×2, ±2 beyond. Chosen over QFD's 1-3-9 and normalised utilities or TOPSIS: readable against a datum, set by the numbers, and no column changes when another candidate is added.
- **The main-job gate**: more than ×2 the datum's error at following (steps or move) or rejecting d does not fit, a −− on a main-job row. **Interpretation**: ×2, where the prototype's competing candidates stay at ×1.6 or below and the clearly worse lie at ×2.1 and above; Hu–Lin (×7.3) no longer wins.
- **Reverse sensitivity**: one weight at a time (the tie point for each of three runners-up), all weights together (the smallest change, as a share), and 4000 seeded draws within one step (SMAA's rank-1 acceptability). **Interpretations**: reasonable means each weight within ±1 of its default and above 0; *robust* means no reasonable single change ties a runner-up and the winner takes ≥ 75 % of the draws, else *sensitive*.
- **"Measure it instead" by matching**: the sensor for an observer is the block output whose run matches the measured signal's rate within 5 %, so it works on the Servo motor main now has (the structure trace of the first prototype found no integrator there).
- **Results**: sliding-mode motor, the boundary-layer sliding mode (+10, robust); oven, the PID, sensitive (the PI from the plant ties it, the feedforward ties if complexity's weight drops to 0.75); tank, keep the loaded schedule (robust); deadbeat motor, state feedback (+16, robust), Hu–Lin, LQR, the planned swing-up and the relay fail the main job.
