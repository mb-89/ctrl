## 2026-10-24 · v2.7: designs are computed in the page ([input](../input/2026-10-24-09-analysis-autotune-triple-swingup.md))

The person: "I really don't want you to store stuff that is specific to any plant into the HTML … It should be computable in principle … the optimizer, it should be part of the package that we deliver, not something that you bake in." The architecture is in [designs.md](designs.md) (condensed) and [designs-in-the-page.md](designs-in-the-page.md) (the full plan from a three-proposal, three-judge panel). It is built in steps; the status is in designs.md.

Rules:
- **Inputs only, in the file and in the library.** A project, `plants.js` and the HTML hold design inputs (weights, times, levels, limits, names), physics parameters, and hand gains a person chose (PID gains, energy swing-up gains). Never stored: gains computed from the plant, tables, trim values (u0, a pipe's start flow), models, plans. (Person.) "Copy as numbers" is the person's explicit way to turn a design into hand numbers; it is offered for LQR, PI and Kalman, never for plans.
- **Same inputs, same numbers.** A computed number depends only on the diagram, the variant's parameters and Ts, and is computed by bundled code; Node and the page give the same numbers. No wall-clock stopping criteria (iteration caps only), no warm starts across edits, no randomness.
- **Caches are memory-only and invisible.** Dropping one changes timing, never results. No plan is kept in localStorage.
- **Laws know nothing of plants.** A law is made from plain data (`make(p, Ts, values)`); only `design(p, ctx)` sees the plant.
- **A failed design never breaks the display.** With no values a Controller puts out u = 0 (an Observer: zero estimates), and the status says why, naming states and measurements by their labels.
- **One plant, one evaluation.** A design sees the plant through the same compiled diagram that is simulated (a `model` compile mode), never through a second copy of the physics, so a design can never be made for a different plant from the one that runs.
- **Dead time's start value may be empty** ("its input at t = 0": the pipe was already flowing), so the tank's pipe no longer carries a baked start flow.

Interpretations:
- "Computed" covers everything derived from the plant: gains, tables, trim values (u0, a pipe's start flow), models and plans. Hand gains, energy-pump gains and physics are inputs.
- A design sees the plant with other Controllers holding their output, so cascades are designed with the inner loop open (the status says so). It sees through Kalman observers (estimate k counts as the true state k) and holds hand observers.
- Dead time is left out of state-space designs and reported. Quantisers and noise are left out of LQR and used for the Kalman R.
- Plans are computed when a system loads (visible; about 3 s in Node, 5 to 7 s in a headless browser after v2.7.1) and are not kept across page loads. A plan that failed still runs, so the attempt can be seen.
- Hand types stay for people who want numbers. "Copy as numbers" is the bridge, offered for LQR, PI and Kalman, never for plans.
- The heavy job (the triple-pendulum swing-up plan) runs on the main thread in short slices, not in a Web Worker: the sandboxed page would likely refuse a worker, and two code paths for one job are not worth it until a design passes about 10 s.

## 2026-10-24 · v2.8: the step method on integrating plants ([input](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md))

- **The step method works on integrating plants** ([autotune.md](autotune.md), *Step response*). (Person, [input](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md): "the step response tuning on the DC motor doesn't seem to work at all. What's up with that?") What was up: the motor's angle integrates its speed, so its step response never settles, and the test refused after four periods (with the motor 68 rad on). Now a ramp is recognised (the level does not settle, its slope does), **the test ends once the slope is steady** (and, since the check below, its lag has faded: 0.38 s after the step on the motor), y may move at most half of r's span (interpretation) and the notes say both, and an integrator with lag and dead time is fitted: k' from the slope, the apparent dead time from the asymptote's intercept, the lag by least squares. **SIMC's integrating rules** (Skogestad 2003, Table 1, checked against the paper): Kc = 1/(k'(τc + θ)), τI = 4(τc + θ), τD = τ2 in his series form, converted to our parallel PID by his eq. 36; a PID when the lag is longer than the dead time (his recommendation), else a PI with the half rule. Motor ×0.10, *Following a sine* ×0.14 of the running variant's cost (×0.096 and ×0.078 before the corrections below, with less robust gains).
- **The step test starts where the loop works**: the running variant holds r0 until y settles, then u is held and stepped (interpretation; before, it stepped from the plant's start, and on the tank that meant from 0.15 m into an overflow). A variant that keeps swinging there (the oven's) hands over at t = 0, as before, and the notes say the response is then exact only for a linear plant. A step that would move y too far is made smaller.
- **"Settled" is read through ripple**: the response averaged over the period of its slowest strong ripple must stay within 2 % of its size; the ripple's amplitude goes into the notes. With ripple on y, **τc is at least the ripple's period** so the loop does not chase it (ours). The encoder example now tunes (×0.93; before it refused, its speed rippling by ±10.7 rad/s). Interpretation: SIMC's τc = θ there gave a loop that sat at its voltage limits a quarter of the time.
- **A gain-scheduled PI gets a schedule from the step method**: a step test at each point of its table (r held there first, the step toward the table's middle, y moving at most half the way to the next point), SIMC PI gains per point, u0 the trim at the start level; one result variant. A point whose test fails takes its nearest neighbour's gains and says why (the tank's 1 m point: a full tank overflows and does not respond). On the tank: ×1.6 of the hand-designed schedule (SIMC's shorter integral times overshoot its reference steps 12 %), against ×23 for the single PI before; it is added, not run.
- **SIMC τc is a choice in the tab: Tight (τc = θ, Skogestad's rule) or Smooth (τc = 3θ)**; `--simc` on the command line; Smooth names its result "· SIMC smooth". Skogestad gives only "τc > θ" for smooth control: **the factor 3 is ours** (interpretation).
- **A result that scores worse says why, in plain words, as far as it can be known** (found while checking the person's report: the tank's ×23 result came with only its cost ratio): what the result cannot do that the running variant does (one PID where gains are scheduled; no derivative), a rough estimate, y's ripple passing through Kp, then the part of the cost that grew most.
