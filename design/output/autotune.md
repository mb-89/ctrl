## Strategies: what exists, what is here

| Family | Well-known members | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Optimise a performance index by simulation | Minimise IAE, ITAE or ISE of the error over a test run, with a derivative-free search such as Nelder–Mead (1965) | **Iterate** |
| Relay feedback, then ultimate-cycle rules | Åström & Hägglund (1984) relay auto-tuner, as in industrial controllers' "autotune"; rules by Ziegler & Nichols (1942), Tyreus & Luyben (1992), and the "no overshoot" row of the Ziegler–Nichols tables | **Relay** |
| Step test (process reaction curve), then model-based rules | Ziegler–Nichols open loop (1942), Cohen–Coon (1953), IMC-PID (Rivera, Morari & Skogestad 1986), SIMC (Skogestad 2003), AMIGO (Åström & Hägglund 2004) | **Step response** (SIMC, for plants that settle and plants that integrate) |
| Iterate on the real plant | Iterative feedback tuning (Hjalmarsson et al. 1998), extremum seeking | Later, with a real (HIL) plant |
| Adaptive control | Self-tuning regulators, model-reference adaptive control | Not planned |

Interpretation: the person asked for "common auto-tuning strategies"; these three cover the three families an engineer meets first (optimisation, relay, step test), and the first one is the "over a number of iterations" the person described.

## The goal: the cost of one run

The tuner runs the loop over **one period of the input** (every reference step and disturbance in it; the screen may be zoomed elsewhere), sampled as the display samples it. It reads the block's own signals: r (input 1), y (input 2) and u. The cost J, smaller is better, is a plain number:

| Part | Formula |
|---|---|
| Error | IAE: mean \|e\|/R; ITAE: mean (τ/T̄)·\|e\|/R; ISE: mean (e/R)². e = r − y; R the span of r (or the largest error when r does not move); τ the time since the last reference step, T̄ the mean time between steps |
| Effort | weight × mean (u − ū)² / U²: the swing of u about its mean; U the actuator range (or the swing of u in the starting run when there are no limits) |
| Overshoot | 2 × Σ over reference steps of the overshoot beyond the bound (as a fraction of the step), measured until the next step or until another input (d) jumps |
| Saturation | 0.2 × the fraction of the time u sits at a limit |

- The **disturbance d plays in the run**, so rejecting it counts in the error.
- A run that **diverges** costs 100–200 (the earlier, the more), a large but finite number, so the search can still find its way back.
- Interpretation: the brief said effort = mean(u²). That counts the power needed to hold a setpoint (an oven at 60 °C needs 60 %), which no tuning can change; the swing about the mean is what a tuner can change. And the recovery from a disturbance is not counted as a step's overshoot: it pushed the optimiser to a weak integral.
- **The goal is fixed for a run**: it is copied when Start is pressed (the core copies it too), and the tab's goal, method and option controls are locked until the run ends. Changing the cost under a running search would compare costs of two different goals.

Settings in the tab: cost type (IAE, ITAE, ISE), effort weight (default 0.05), overshoot bound (default 10 %). They belong to the page session, not the project file (yet).

## Iterate: Nelder–Mead

- **What it turns**, as log factors on the variant's own values (x = 0 is the variant as it is; ln 2 doubles a gain), bounded to ×1/1000 … ×1000. Signs never flip; a gain at 0 stays off, because the structure is the person's choice. PID: Kp, Ki, Kd and the reference feedforward Kff, Kv, Ka. Gain-scheduled PI: the Kp table and the Ki table, each scaled as a whole, and Kff, Kv, Ka. State feedback: each gain in K and the prefilter kr. Sliding mode: λ, and φ when it is above 0 (a relay stays a relay); **not K**, the switching gain: the cost counts the time u sits at a limit, and a K just under the limit (12 → 11.1 V on *DC motor, sliding mode*) zeroed that count while the relay still slammed, with more heat (7.6 → 13.4 W while holding). The cost has no chattering term (no V/s), so it does not judge chattering. Super-twisting: λ, k1 and k2; tuned on the nominal motor (λ 21, k1 2.5, k2 83) they lose the load at J×3 (368 mrad peak under it, 28 W while holding), because the cost sees only the plant as it is. Off, swing-up, relay and the planned swing-up say why they cannot be tuned. **A variant from the plant** (LQR, PI from the plant, swing-up with an LQR catch; [designs.md](designs.md)) is tuned as its Copy as numbers twin (state feedback, PID or gain-scheduled PI, energy swing-up), starting from the numbers its design computes now; the result is a hand variant beside it, and the designed one stays and follows the plant. A design with no numbers (refused, or a plan not made yet) says so (decisions v2.7.2). Only the running variant's own parameters are tuned, not its structure or the other variants (the person: "I wonder if autotune currently goes only over the current control architecture … I guess that's okay").
- **Feedforward** (the person asked for it in the standard controller, [controller-proposal.md](controller-proposal.md)): a feedforward gain that is not 0 is turned like every other gain. **Feedforward: tune those at 0 too** (an option of Iterate; `--ff`, `feedforward: true`) also turns Kff, Kv and Ka that are 0. A log factor cannot move a 0, so each starts from a value read from the starting run: the feedforward should put out the u the loop needs to follow r (rf: r through the variant's prefilter), and that has two parts:
  - **Kff**, what holds y at r: the fit u ≈ c + Kff·rf over the samples where rf rests and y has settled at it (within 2 % of r's span). It needs two levels of r at rest; a sine never rests, so it stays 0. An integrating plant needs no u to stay anywhere, so on the square-wave motor Kff comes out 0, as it should (a fit over the whole run read 1.16 V/rad from the transients, a start with a steady error of 0.12 rad); on the oven 0.78.
  - **Kv, Ka**, what moving takes: u − Kff·rf ≈ c + Kv·drf/dt + Ka·d²rf/dt² over the whole run.
  - Samples where another input (the disturbance d) is not 0 are left out: there u answers the disturbance. A term whose signal the run cannot tell from the ones before it (a constant r has no speed) is left out, and so is one that explains less than 2 % of u's swing: that gain stays 0 and is not turned.
  - On *Following a sine (XY)*, the PID ticked: the fit reads Kff 0, Kv 0.73 V·s/rad and Ka 0.032 V·s²/rad, close to the motor's 0.7 and 0.042 (a sine's acceleration is no longer mistaken for a static gain, which only fitted 0.2 Hz). The search takes Kv to 1.7 (the motor's 0.7 plus Kd) and the cost from 0.011 to 0.0005 (without the option 0.0021).
  - Interpretation, the cleanest rule we found: what is not 0 is the person's structure and is tuned; the option adds the feedforward structure, starting where the run says it should, and the search then refines it toward the goal (the fit sees the u the plant needs, not what the D part on y takes away, which the search adds).
- **Feedforward first, then the feedback**: with feedforward and feedback gains to tune, Iterate runs two searches, shown as one run: the feedforward with the feedback held (at most half the iterations), then the feedback with the feedforward held. A feedforward cannot change the loop, so the first is always safe. **A run without a disturbance cannot judge the feedback once a feedforward follows r**: tuned together on the sine, 250 iterations took *PID + feedforward* to Kp 3.7, Ki 2.1 (three times the error under a 0.3 N·m load, the slowest pole at −0.6/s instead of −3.3/s); tuned after the feedforward, Ki still fell from 80 to 0.09. So when the run has no disturbance and a feedforward is on after its search, the feedback is held and the result says so ("give d a step in the Input tab, or set the feedforward to 0 first"); when the feedforward search ends with all its gains at 0, the feedback is tuned as without the option. With a disturbance (the square-wave motor's load pulse, the oven's open door) both searches run. Interpretation: the review's "tune the feedback first, then the feedforward" would on this cost tune the feedback for following without help (the sine's PID ended at a phase margin of 5°, Ms 13.6); holding the feedback is what a run without a disturbance can honestly support.
- **The search**: Nelder–Mead simplex (reflect, expand, contract, shrink) from a simplex of steps ln 2. When the simplex collapses it restarts around the best point with smaller steps, twice at most. It stops after the maximum number of iterations (60) or when 15 iterations have not lowered the cost by 0.1 %. Deterministic: the same project gives the same gains.
- **Pacing: the header's Speed** ([instrument.md](instrument.md#speed)). Every method is a generator that yields after **each run of the loop**, also inside an iteration (a shrink runs the loop n times), and says how much time that run simulated. At a finite speed **every run Iterate tries sweeps across the display at that speed and lasts its own length**, one period of the input, whatever the screen shows, as a run on a real plant would take its time: a 5 s run takes 5 s at 1×, 0.5 s at 10×, 50 s at 0.1×, on a 1 s screen too (it sweeps the screen's part of it and goes on beyond it) and on a 10 s screen (it ends at 5 s). **The next run is computed while one sweeps** (Nelder–Mead needs only a run's cost, known before its sweep), in slices, and starts in the frame the one before ends, carrying the rest of that frame, so no time is lost between runs: 10× reads 10× and the whole tuning runs at 9.5–9.8×. At **∞** it computes as fast as it can, in slices of about 30 ms (a slice stops before the next step would overrun it), so the page keeps drawing, and the readout beside Speed shows the simulated seconds it computes per second. An improvement (its gains into the project, the page redrawn) is a task of its own, at most four times a second at ∞; the tuner's run with those gains stands for the screen's own when the screen is one period of the input (no second simulation). **The display stands still for every method** while it runs, so at ∞ the readout counts the tuner's computing. A run on the display is paced by the page's frames, so it pauses while the browser tab is hidden, as the sweep does (the computing ahead stops at the next run). The person: "the fast toggle of the autotune, that would go away. If I want to autotune fast, then I just set the toggle to higher speeds" ([input 10](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md)); the **Fast** switch is gone. On the oven (77 runs of 5 s) 10× took 41 s and ∞ 2–3 s; at 1× the runs alone take 6½ minutes (measurements in [instrument.md](instrument.md#speed)). **Unlimited speed** (the person, [input 45](../input/2026-10-25-45-autotune-unlimited-speed.md)): a key beside Start, off by default. Pressed, a run sets the header's Speed to ∞ and, when it ends (done or stopped), back to the speed set before, unless someone set another meanwhile. It is not offered with a live plant, which holds 1×.
- On the oven it takes the cost from 1.24 to 0.111 (×0.09) in 44 iterations (98 runs of its 10 s cycle; on the 5 s run before inputs 50–52, 1.28 to 0.12 in 37): the ringing goes, overshoot falls from 42 % to the 10 % bound. The motor's aggressive P goes from Kp 30 to 6 (×0.09).

## Relay: Åström–Hägglund

1. **Operating point**: the Source that feeds r is found back along the wires, through gains, lags and subsystem ports, and held at the middle of its own range; every other Source (d) is held at 0; **r0 is the r this gives** (read from the run). The running variant holds r0 first; the u it needs there is the bias u0. Without such a Source (r made from several signals) the experiment runs with the inputs as they are and says the estimate is rough.
2. **Direction**: a short open-loop step (u held at u0 and at u0 ± d) shows which way y goes. If y falls when u rises (a reverse-acting plant), the relay is reversed (negative d), Ku comes out negative and so do the gains, as they must.
3. **Relay**: the controller is replaced by a relay, u = u0 ± d, switching when e passes ±ε. d is a fraction of the actuator range (default 10 %; the range is the variant's limits, else the swing of u in its own run; with neither, the test refuses and asks for limits); ε is 0.1 % of r's span. The relay is kept inside the limits, so it is symmetric about u0; with less than a tenth of the asked swing left, the bias is at a limit and the test refuses.
4. **Bias correction**: if the relay is not high half the time, u0 becomes the mean of u over the last cycle and the test runs again (up to six times); if it never switches, u0 moves by d.
5. **Read the cycle**: the run grows (×4, up to 16 input periods) until it holds four cycles. Pu is the mean period of the last cycles; a is the amplitude of the fundamental of y over the last cycle (a Fourier coefficient: the describing function assumes a sine); **Ku = 4d / (π √(a² − ε²))**, the describing function of a relay with hysteresis (Åström & Hägglund, *PID Controllers: Theory, Design, and Tuning*, 1995).
6. **Rule** (Ti = Kp/Ki, Td = Kd/Kp):

| Rule | Kp | Ti | Td | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ziegler–Nichols (1942) | 0.6 Ku | Pu / 2 | Pu / 8 | Fast, about a quarter decay; overshoots |
| Tyreus–Luyben (1992) | Ku / 2.2 (≈ 0.45 Ku) | 2.2 Pu | Pu / 6.3 | Calmer, more robust (the default) |
| No overshoot | 0.2 Ku | Pu / 2 | Pu / 3 | Gentle; the row usually tabulated with Ziegler–Nichols. Despite its name it overshoots on integrating plants (the motor: 44 %) |

The new PID keeps the running variant's limits, start output, setpoint weight, prefilter, feedforward and anti-windup; its derivative is filtered at Tf = Td/10, but never shorter than a controller sample Ts (on the motor, Ts: a shorter filter would pass the sampling's steps almost unfiltered). A feedforward that is on moves with the new gains, so it keeps doing its job: Kv by the change of Kd, Kff by the change of Kp(1 − b) (a feedforward that removes the following error holds the plant's need plus what D and P(b) take away, [controller-proposal.md](controller-proposal.md)). On the sine's *PID + feedforward*, Tyreus–Luyben sets Kd 0.614 and Kv becomes 1.31; keeping 1.7 left the settled error 7 times larger.

**When it cannot run, it says why**: a block with more than one measurement (state feedback: use Iterate); an input not wired; no actuator range; the bias at an actuator limit; the relay never switched; too few cycles (a slow plant: lengthen the input's period); an oscillation no larger than the hysteresis (an integrator or first-order lag alone has no ultimate point); a diverging run (unstable without its controller). Drift, an unsettled cycle, asymmetry and a reduced amplitude are noted with the result.

**Accuracy**: the describing function is an approximation (the research notes give −19 % … +27 % on Ku depending on the plant). For K/(s+1)³ with K = 8 (Ku = 1, Pu = 2π/√3 ≈ 3.63 s) the test finds Ku within 6 % and Pu within 3 % (about 0.95 and 3.72 s at Ts = 10 ms: the relay cycle sits slightly off the −180° point, and sampling adds half a sample of delay); with K = −8 it finds Ku ≈ −0.94.

## Step response: SIMC

Revised 2026-10-24 after the person found it did nothing on the DC motor ([input](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md): "the step response tuning on the DC motor doesn't seem to work at all"). It refused there because the motor's angle never settles: it integrates the speed. It now handles integrating plants, reads through ripple, and builds a gain schedule.

**The experiment.** r's Source is held at the middle of its range, as for the relay, and the running variant holds r0 **until y has settled** within 2 % of how far it moved, or of r's span: y averaged over its ripple (below) stays within that band, and its swing about the average has come down to the swing it keeps at the end (a steady ripple, noise), give or take the same band. The average alone would also pass a loop still ringing down (found in the check: the claim "until y settles" needs y itself in the band). On the motor the step comes at 0.89 s either way, after the last swing of its P beyond the band (0.047 rad at 0.86 s; the band is 0.04 rad, 2 % of r's span); for a linear plant the difference of the two runs cancels what is left of the ringing. Then the controller is replaced by a constant: u0 in one run (what the variant needed at r0), u0 + Δu in a second (default 10 % of the range; upwards if there is room). **Their difference dy, from the switch on, is the plant's step response from that state** (exact for a linear plant; for a nonlinear one it starts where the loop works). A variant that keeps swinging at r0 (the badly tuned oven sits in a limit cycle) cannot hand over a plant at rest; the test then starts one sample after t = 0 from the plant's own start (the variant runs that first sample, so a dead time whose output before T_d is its input at t = 0, the tank's pipe, holds what the loop put out there and passes the step after T_d; stepping at t = 0 read the pipe's 0.1 s as 0.013 s), and the notes say it is exact only for a linear plant. Interpretation: before, the test always started at t = 0, which on the conical tank stepped from 0.15 m and overflowed the tank; holding r first tests the plant where the loop works.

**How far y may move.** Half of r's span (r0 is its middle): a step that would move y further is made smaller (to 80 % of what fits, at least halved; five times at most), and the notes say so. What fits is judged from where y settles, or, when it has not settled, from how far it got (a ramp: how far it would get by the time the test needs); before, such a step was only halved, three times, and a step size other than the default broke the tank (25 %: cost ×5.1; "PI tuned for empty" refused from 25 % on). Now the tank's schedule costs ×1.6–2.0 of the hand-made one for any step from 5 to 100 %, and its single PIs tune at every size. Interpretation: the test should stay in the range the loop works in; a large step on a nonlinear plant fits an average of several operating points (the tank's fixed PI gave θ = 0 that way).

**Settled, robustly.** dy has settled when, **averaged over the period of its ripple**, it stays within 2 % of the size of the response for the last fifth of the record. The ripple's period is the slowest strong periodic part of the last 40 % of the record (Hann-windowed spectrum, trend removed; peaks at least a quarter of the largest, six periods or more in the stretch; none when the largest is under 0.2 % of the response). The average is taken twice (a triangle two periods long), so the ripple's harmonics drop out too. Steady ripple and noise count as settled; their amplitude (half the peak-to-peak about the average) goes into the notes. Interpretation: the encoder example's step response carries ±10.7 rad/s of encoder error (17 % of the 64 rad/s step), 1× and 2× at two speeds and the shaft resonance; a raw 2 % band never passed, so the test refused although the speed follows V with a time constant of 0.07 s.

**A ramp: an integrating plant.** If dy does not settle but **its slope does** (the secant over the ripple period, and over at least 2 % of the record, stays within 2 % of its final value for the last fifth, and that slope accounts for at least half of how far y went), the plant integrates. **The test then ends once the slope is steady and the lag has faded**: first where the last fifth of the record lies on the steady slope; then, as the fit there gives L and τ2, the record runs on until its last fifth starts L + 5 τ2 after the step (the lag's transient e^(−t/τ2) under 1 %), and is fitted again. The plant does not run away: where y would leave its bound before then, the step is made smaller (by how far y would get at that time). The display shows the cut run and the notes say when it ended and how far y moved ("the test ended 0.38 s after the step … y moved by 0.549 rad (it may move 1 rad, half of r's span)"). Before the revision, the test ran four periods and refused (the motor turned 68 rad). Why the record runs on (found in the check): cut where the slope first looked steady (0.26 s on the motor), the line through the last fifth still carried about 1 % of the lag; k' came out 1 % low, L 4 % short, θ 22 % long and τ2 10 % short.

**Models.**

| Response | Model | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Settles | First order plus dead time, K e^(−θs)/(τs + 1) | Smith's two-point method on dy averaged over its ripple period (without lag): K = Δy∞/Δu, τ = 1.5 (t63 − t28), θ = t63 − τ. If that θ is less than half the time y takes to move 2 %, θ is that time and τ = t63 − θ (interpretation: a response that is no first order, such as the tank stepped far, gave θ = 0, which no plant with a pipe has) |
| Ramps | Integrator with lag and dead time, k' e^(−θs)/(s (τ2 s + 1)) | The least-squares line through the last fifth gives the slope k'·Δu (k' per unit of u) and, where it crosses the start level, the apparent dead time L = θ + τ2 (the asymptote's intercept). τ2 is the lag in [0, L] that fits the whole response best (least squares: a scan, then golden section); θ = L − τ2 |

On a known plant, 2 e^(−0.3s)/(s (0.2s + 1)), the test finds k' 2.00, L 0.496 s, θ 0.300 s, τ2 0.196 s (k' and θ within 0.2 %; before the record ran on: k' 1.98, θ 0.307 s, τ2 0.18 s). On the motor it finds k' 1.427 rad/s per V (the motor's 0.5 · 10 / (1 + 0.5 · 0.5 · 10) = 1.429), τ2 48.8 ms, θ 10.8 ms (its speed has two lags, 48.1 ms and 11.9 ms: armature and mechanics coupled by the back-EMF; one lag fitted by least squares to a long record gives τ2 49.7 ms, θ 10.3 ms). The motor's test now uses Δu = 1.2 V: at 2.4 V the angle would leave its bound of 1 rad before the lag had faded.

**SIMC rules** (Skogestad 2003, *Simple analytic rules for model reduction and PID controller tuning*, J. Process Control 13: 291–309, Table 1, checked against the paper). θ includes half a controller sample (Ts/2), the delay of the hold on u (his eq. 10–11); for a PID, also the derivative's filter (below).

| Model | Kc (series) | τI | τD |
|---|---|---|---|
| First order plus dead time | τ / (K (τc + θ)) | min(τ, 4 (τc + θ)) | – |
| Integrating | 1 / (k' (τc + θ)) | 4 (τc + θ) | – |
| Integrating with lag | 1 / (k' (τc + θ)) | 4 (τc + θ) | τ2 |

The PID is in Skogestad's series form; this code's PID is parallel, so (his eq. 36) Kp = Kc (1 + τD/τI), Ki = Kc/τI, Kd = Kc τD. Check: the numbers of his example of eq. 36 in the appendix (series Kc 0.5, τI 1, τD 1 is ideal Kc' 1, τI' 2, τD' 0.5; there for e^(−s)/(s + 1)²) are a test, here from an integrating plant with the same series settings. **The derivative is used when the lag is longer than the dead time** (τ2 > θ: Skogestad recommends PID "for processes with dominant second order dynamics (with τ2 > θ, approximately)"); otherwise a PI, with half of τ2 counted as dead time (his half rule). A first order plus dead time gives a PI, as before.

**The derivative's filter is dead time.** The derivative is filtered at Tf = Td/10, but never shorter than a controller sample (as for the relay's PID). Skogestad's margins (for an integrating process with τc = θ: Ms 1.70, gain margin 2.96, phase margin 46.9°, his Table 2) are computed without a derivative filter (his simulations use 0.01 τD); his rules count no lag in the controller but the sampling's Ts/2. On the motor, Tf = Ts = 10 ms, a quarter of Td and about as long as θ itself, so **Tf joins θ** (a lag shorter than the one kept, counted whole as his half rule counts such lags; Tf depends on the gains, so the rule repeats until it holds), and the notes say so ("θ = 0.0158 + 0.01 = 0.0258 s"). Found when SIMC was checked against the paper: the formulas were right, but on the sampled motor loop the gains from before (Kp 26.6, Ki 143, Kd 0.91) had Ms 2.46, gain margin 2.27 and phase margin 32° (a 0.02 rad step overshot 33 %; now 22 %, the overshoot two integrators give); the library's experiment could not show it (its 2 rad steps keep u at a limit 10 % of the time). Now: Kp 16.8, Ki 65.8, Kd 0.66, **Ms 1.76, gain margin 3.4, phase margin 49°** on the exact sampled motor; on an integrator with a lag of 5 samples and no dead time Ms 1.70 (a test), where the rule without the filter in θ gives Ms about 10. Interpretation: the half sample of the derivative's backward difference is not counted as well; with it the motor's loop was more robust than SIMC aims for (Ms 1.58, gain margin 4.2) and slower (cost ×0.115 against ×0.102).

**τc, the one knob**, offered in the tab as **Tight** (τc = θ, Skogestad's "SIMC-rule for fast response with good robustness", his eq. 27) or **Smooth** (τc = 3θ). Skogestad gives only "τc > θ" for smooth control (smoother input, less noise sensitivity, more robustness); **the factor 3 is ours** (interpretation). Two floors, both ours, on the θ that sets τc (not on the θ in the gain): at least τ/10 (almost no dead time, as before) and **at least the ripple's period** when y ripples, so the loop does not chase the ripple (interpretation: on the encoder, τc = τ/10 = 6.7 ms gave Kp 0.9, which keeps u at its limits a quarter of the time and scores ×7.2 of the running PI; τc = 42.5 ms, the ripple's period, gives Kp 0.21 and ×0.93). The notes say when a floor set τc ("τc = 0.0919 s is set from a tenth of the lag τ = 0.919 s (our floor), not from the dead time θ = 0.0856 s, which is shorter"), and the tab shows **the τc used and what set it** (θ, 3θ, τ/10, the ripple's period; the full sentence in its tooltip), for each point of a schedule too. Before, the tab's row said "τc = θ" also where a floor had set it.

**Two integrators overshoot.** With a PI on an integrating plant, the integral of r − y after a step in r must come back to zero, so y overshoots. When it overshoots beyond the goal's bound, one more run tries the result with a prefilter Tr = τI on r (it cancels the PI's zero; Skogestad: "the setpoint response can always be modified by introducing a 'feedforward' filter on the setpoint") and the notes say what it would give, as cost ratios of the running variant with and without it ("Smooth" on the motor: 14.3 % → 0 %, cost ×0.26 of the running P against ×0.37 without; before, the ratio was against the result, which read like a gain over what runs when it was not). The prefilter is not set: on *Following a sine* it would lag the sine (cost ×6.5).

**A gain-scheduled PI gets a schedule.** When the running variant is a gain-scheduled PI, the step test runs **at each of its points** (the table's y values): the running variant holds r there first (four periods when y has not settled in one; r's Source is set to the target, and once more on the line through the operating point when r is not the Source's own value), then the step. Each step goes **toward the middle of the table** (the first test, nearest the middle, steps u up and tells which way y goes), using what room u has that way (the other way only with less than a tenth of the step's size), and **may move y half the way to the next point**, so each model is local. Each point gets SIMC PI gains (a gain-scheduled PI has no derivative); a point whose test fails takes the nearest working point's gains, and the notes say why. **u0**, the PI's integral part at the start, is the trim at the start level: what the running variant needs to hold y where the plant starts. One result variant, "· SIMC", with the same table points, prefilter, limits, feedforward and anti-windup (the scheduled PI has no D and b = 1, so nothing moves its feedforward). On the tank the points 0.1 … 0.95 m give K 1.54 … 4.33 and τ 0.052 … 2.9 s, as the tank widens (θ 0.076 … 0.18 s). The library's schedule is a PI from the plant whose levels top out at 0.95 m (decisions v2.7.2), where the step test works too; a hand schedule with a point at 1 m (the brim) shows what happens there: the tank is full and overflows: a step of −0.3 drains it beyond its bound, a smaller one leaves it overflowing, and the point takes 0.75 m's gains with that reason ("y does not respond to a step of u (Δu = −0.124) at r0 = 1 m, though a step of Δu = −0.3 moved it by more than 0.125 m: the plant sits at a limit of its own there"; before, it asked whether the controller's output was wired, which other points had shown it was); u0 = 0.172 = a √(2 g · 0.15 m).

## The tab

Four columns: **Controller** (the block, if several; the running variant and its gains; where the result goes, or in red why it cannot run), **Method** (Iterate, Relay, Step response, with their settings: Iterate's "Feedforward: tune those at 0 too" for a PID or scheduled PI; the relay's rule; the step's SIMC τc, Tight or Smooth; the size of the relay or step), **Goal** (cost type, effort weight, overshoot bound), **Progress** (Start/Stop, status, a cost-against-iteration plot, and before → after: cost, overshoot, gains, Ku and Pu, or the step test's model: K, τ, θ, or k′, τ2, θ, or one row per point of a schedule, and the τc used with what set it: θ, 3θ, τ/10 or the ripple's period). How fast it runs is the header's Speed; the **Unlimited speed** key beside Start runs one tune at ∞ and gives the Speed back after. One short line per column; what a method or the cost is lives in tooltips and the help dialog (?). When the columns stack (narrow screens), Progress with Start comes first.

- **Results go into a variant of their own**: "<original> · auto-tuned" for Iterate, "· relay T–L" (or Z–N, no overshoot) and "· SIMC" (or "· SIMC smooth") for the others, after the original in the block's variant list. Interpretation: one name per method, so the rules can be compared by flipping variants in the Controller tab. **An earlier result is never overwritten**: running a method again from the original makes "… · auto-tuned 2". Tuning again from a result updates that result in place (the target line says "Updates …").
- **Iterate** creates its variant at the first improvement (none if nothing improves) and runs it; each better set of gains replaces it.
- **A relay or step result that scores worse than the running variant is added but does not run**: the status says so with the cost ratio and **why, in plain words, as far as it can be known**, and the original keeps running (flip to the result in the Controller tab to see it). Why: first what the result cannot do that the running variant does ("one PID from a relay test at h = 0.475 m; Gain-scheduled PI changes its gains with h (Kp 0.113 … 2.87), which a single PID cannot"; a PI where the running PID has a derivative), that the experiment's estimate was rough, that y's ripple passes through Kp into u (when that moves u by 5 % of its range or more); then the part of the cost that grew most ("it overshoots 19.4 % (the goal allows 10 %; Gain-scheduled PI: 7.61 %)", "r − y is larger (IAE ×2.22)", u swings more, u sits at a limit). The command line and `--json` (`worse`) say the same. Interpretation: before, the person was told only the cost ratio; we say what is measured or structural, never a guess about the plant (a relay test at one level cannot tell that the tank's gain changes with the level, so the text does not claim it).
- **A relay or step result that is unstable where the run starts is not run either, whatever its run scores**, where the running variant is stable there (v2.7.3). The design layer starts the tank in exact balance (u0 computed from the plant), and a loop in exact balance rests even where it is unstable, until r or d moves it: the relay's PID, with six closed-loop poles outside the unit circle at h = 0.15 m, ran still until the step at 1.5 s and cost ×1.01 of the gain-scheduled PI (with the 0.172 stored before, it rang from t = 0 and cost ×3.6). So the result's loop is also linearised at the start state (`loopModel` at `'initial'`, `analyzeLoop`), and the status reads "The result is not run (cost ×0.95 against Gain-scheduled PI): it is unstable where the run starts (the initial state, q = 0.172: 6 closed-loop poles outside the unit circle); the run rests there only because it starts in exact balance, and the first disturbance there sets it off; …". Where the result's run scores better (the tank's relay PID since v2.7.4), the rest is what the result itself gives (one PID where the running variant schedules its gains), not a cost that did not grow. Nothing is said where that cannot be judged (no linear law, a kink, u at a limit: the oven starts with its heater at 0). `--json` carries `unstableAtStart: { Z, at }`. Not done for Iterate: its runs start in the same balance, so a gain set unstable at the start level scores as if it were not; on the library none is (each Iterate result is stable at its start).
- **Watching**: at a finite speed each run Iterate tries sweeps across the display (as a Single), the best runs so far faded behind it (the display's five-run memory); when the sweep ends, a better run becomes the best (its gains go into the result variant) and the next run sweeps. The status says which run and iteration is on the display and at what speed. At ∞ the display stands still and shows each improvement whole, the run before fading, up to four a second. The cost plot (dark, log scale) shows the best cost so far (green) and every run tried (blue dots). The relay and step tests compute first, as fast as they can (the status counts the runs; they are not shown), then **sweep their experiment across the display at the Speed, labelled as the experiment** in the status (the relay shows its last period, where the cycle has settled; the step test from a tenth of a period before the step, with the hold and the step in words; the experiment lasts its own length at the speed, so the motor's 0.9 s ramp test ends where its record ends; at ∞ at once; in XY, FFT or Spectrogram mode the status says that Time shows the test over time: in XY a held r draws a vertical line), hold it 0.4 s, and then the loop's own run comes back, **whether or not the experiment gave a result**; the result's numbers appear after the sweep. Interpretation: the person's "set the toggle to higher speeds" read as: each run is shown at the speed (so 1× is a run in real time, like tuning a real plant), while the experiments' preparatory runs are computed first, as before.
- **At the end** the display holds the loop's run, with the run it started from faded behind a new result, and the run mode is restored. A toast gives the cost ratio.
- **One undo step for a whole run**: Ctrl+Z restores the project as it was before Start, and the progress column forgets a result that undo removed.
- **Stop** keeps the best gains found so far (Iterate) or applies nothing (experiments). **The scope's Stop does the same while a run of the tuner sweeps on the display**, and the scope stays stopped (it used to skip to the next run). Loading another system or undoing during a run stops it.

## On the identified model

With Identify ([identification.md](identification.md)) the tab tunes on the diagram or on the identified model, one choice per block ("Plant: the diagram | the model", stored as `system.identify[key].source`); a plant outside the page only on the model. The method runs unchanged on a copy with the model in the plant's place and a test push at its input; results are named "… (model)", and on a simulated plant the status gives the cost on the model and on the diagram (the hidden variation's copy when one is set). **Try on the plant** then takes a PI or PID result to the plant in three screened steps, with a monitor that falls back to the controller that held. Details and measurements: identification.md §10.1 and §11.1.

## How the core reads a loop

`compileDiagram` adds, for every Controller block (not Observer blocks), probes under its id path: `ctrl.r` (input 1), `ctrl.y` (input 2), `ctrl.u` (output); inside a subsystem `motor/ctrl.u`. A signal name never has a dot, so no scope channel lists them. `tuneContext` checks that inputs 1 and 2 are wired (else: "wire the measurement y to input 2"), copies the plant and runs variants on the copy (`withVariant`), never touching the project. A plant with an External plant block (Live mode) is refused: offline it reads 0, and every method simulates.

**Auto Scale** uses the probes too (`loopOverlays`): the channels that show a Controller block's r and the y it compares with r (the same values), in one unit, share one scale and offset, so they overlay; nothing else is grouped, and XY mode is left alone. Interpretation: without it the oven's r showed at 10 °C/div and T at 20, and T seemed never to reach r. The person has not seen this yet.
