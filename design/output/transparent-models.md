# Transparent models: the physics blocks become diagrams you can open

Reader: the agent who turns the three black-box physics blocks (Cart–pole, Pendulum chain on cart, Conical tank) into subsystems the person can open, or who adds a physical model to Loop Tuner. This is the plan; **steps 1–4 are built** (2026-10-25, branch `feat/model-foundations`, decisions v2.8 "model foundations"; see §9), steps 4–9 are not. It is the synthesis of a panel run on 2026-10-24: three approaches were prototyped (A: Equations and Linear-solve blocks with visible integrators; B: one Mechanics block holding M(q), V and Q; C: physical components joined at frames) and two judges scored them (C 8/10, A 6–6.5, B 5). This plan takes **C** and grafts A's Equations block and limited Integrator and B's equation sheet, refusals and tests. The numbers below were measured on a scratch copy of `feat/designs` at `6fa5266` with C's compiler patch plus the grafts. Where the code and this plan differ later, the code and [decisions.md](decisions.md) win.

The person ([input 10](../input/2026-10-24-10-sliding-mode-and-triple-swingup.md)): "what I see a lot is that you put like the cart and the three rods, you just put that as a black box model … I want to be able to click into that and see what it's about. Model it in our diagrams. And if you find that there's something you can't model, then add it as a primitive to our diagrams, right? So they shouldn't be black boxes." Later: the rule is for the models we build; an identified model of a real system may be a black box ([decisions.md](decisions.md)).

## 0. Bottom line: what the person sees

**Double-click "cart and 3 rods" in the Triple pendulum and it opens like any subsystem.** Inside are the parts an engineer would draw on a whiteboard:

```
 F ──►┌────────┐ x ───────────────────────────────────────► Out x
      │ Rail   │ v ───────────────────────────────────────► Out v
      │ F − b·v│        θ1, ω1 ─────► Out θ1, ω1   θ2, ω2 ─► …   θ3, ω3 ─► …
      └─▪────▪─┘       ┌───────┐        ┌───────┐        ┌───────┐
 ┌─────┐│    │┌─────┐  │Hinge 1│┌─────┐ │Hinge 2│┌─────┐ │Hinge 3│┌─────┐
 │World├┘    └┤cart ├──┤  θ1   ├┤rod 1├─┤  θ2   ├┤rod 2├─┤  θ3   ├┤rod 3│
 │ g ↓ │      │  M  │  └───────┘│l1,m1│ └───────┘│l2,m2│ └───────┘│l3,m3│
 └─────┘      └─────┘           └─────┘          └─────┘          └─────┘
```

(Arrows: signal wires. ▪ and the plain lines between parts: frame ports and joins.)

- A **World** (gravity g), a **Rail** (the cart's coordinate x and speed v, friction b) carrying the cart, a **Mass** M, then three pairs of **Hinge** θk (rate ωk) and **Rod** lk with its bob mk at the tip.
- The parts are joined end to end by thick grey **joins** (a point that moves as one; no signal flows on it). Thin signal wires take x, θ1…θ3, v, ω1…ω3 from the Rail and the Hinges to eight Out ports.
- Each part's face shows its one-line law: Rail "F − b·v", Hinge "θ from upright", Rod "l, m at tip", World "g ↓".
- **Selecting any part shows the equations of motion**, derived from how the parts are joined, in the names of the subsystem's parameters. For the triple pendulum the first row and the potential read (generated by the prototype):

  ```
  (M + m1 + m2 + m3)·v̇ + l1·(m1 + m2 + m3)·cos θ1·ω̇1 + l2·(m2 + m3)·cos θ2·ω̇2 + l3·m3·cos θ3·ω̇3
      = F − b·v + l1·(m1 + m2 + m3)·sin θ1·ω1² + l2·(m2 + m3)·sin θ2·ω2² + l3·m3·sin θ3·ω3²
  V = g·l1·(m1 + m2 + m3)·cos θ1 + g·l2·(m2 + m3)·cos θ2 + g·l3·m3·cos θ3
  ```

  Hovering a coefficient shows its value for this plant (l1·(m1 + m2 + m3) = 0.075).
- **From outside nothing is lost.** The closed subsystem keeps the same ports (F → x, θ1…θ3, v, ω1…ω3) and the Parameters panel shows the same fields as the old block (M, m1…m3, l1…l3, g, b, start angles): a **mask**. The rates agree with the old block to 1.5·10⁻¹³ (relative), the planned swing-up is the same plan, and it is planned in the same time (median 3.18 s against 3.16 s, §4).

**Double-click "tank" in the Conical tank** and it opens as four ordinary blocks: q (the net flow in: pump less draw-off; v2.7.4, a draw-off larger than the pump's flow drains the tank) → *net inflow* (Σ, + −) → *level rate* (r = r0 + (R − r0)·h/H; A = π·r²; dh = Q/A) → ∫ *h* [0, H] → Out h, and h → *outlet (Torricelli)* (qout = a·√(2·g·h)) back into the Σ. The tank needs no new part: Equations, Sum and an Integrator with limits.

**New building blocks** (each with one formula, §1): five Mechanics parts (World, Rail, Hinge, Mass, Rod) and joins; the **Equations** block (the Function block, with several named inputs and outputs); the **Integrator** with a start value, limits, unit and angle flag; **masks** on subsystems (parameters that may be formulas in the mask's names). Rigid-body constraints are what the existing blocks cannot express without an algebraic loop, hence the parts.

## 1. The new primitives

### 1.1 Mechanics parts (planar), joined at frame ports

| Part | Frame ports | Signal ports | Parameters (defaults) | Law (on its face) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| World | W | none | g (9.81 m/s²) | The fixed ground. Gravity is −g·ŷ. One World per mechanism |
| Rail (prismatic joint) | B (base), F (follower) | in: F (when `force` = 1); out: s, ṡ | `name` (x), `rate` (v), b friction (0 N·s/m), `incline` α (0 rad), x0, v0, `force` (1) | follower = base + s·(cos α, sin α); generalised force F − b·ṡ. Its base must not turn (the World or another rail) |
| Hinge (revolute joint) | B, F | in: τ (when `torque` = 1); out: θ, ω | `name` (θ), `rate` (ω), c damping (0 N·m·s/rad), theta0, omega0, `torque` (0) | θ is the follower's angle from upright, positive towards +x (today's convention). Torque τ − c·(ω − ω_base) acts on the follower; the reaction acts on the base |
| Mass | F | none | m (1 kg) | A point mass at the frame |
| Rod | B, T (tip) | none | l (0.5 m), m (0 kg), `at` (tip \| uniform) | tip = base + l·(sin φ, cos φ). `tip`: m is a bob at the end of a massless rod (today's model). `uniform`: m at l/2 with I = m·l²/12 |

- `name` and `rate` are a new parameter kind `coord`: a short name that may be Greek (θ1, ω1). `at` is a new kind `choice`.
- **Joins** are a new list in a diagram: `"joins": [["world:W", "rail:B"], ["rail:F", "cart:F", "hinge1:B"], …]`. Each join is one point; it has no direction. `normalizeDiagram` refuses a port that does not exist, a port in two joins, and a join of fewer than two ports, each with a sentence. Joins stay inside one diagram level (frame ports on subsystem boundaries are not built, §10).

**How a mechanism compiles** (`core/mech.js`, C's fast form; 239 lines in the prototype). The compile groups each level's joins into frame nodes; parts linked through frame nodes form one mechanism, which must contain exactly one World. For the class handled (trees rooted at the World; rails on parts that do not turn; hinge coordinates as absolute angles) every body's position is

  p_b = c_b + Σ_p s_p·d_p + Σ_j L_bj·(sin θ_j, cos θ_j)

with constant rail directions d_p and constant lengths L_bj (the rods from hinge j to body b). So these are built once from the topology, and per evaluation only one sin and one cos per hinge are taken:

- M_pp' = Σ_b m_b·P_bp·P_bp'·(d_p · d_p')
- M_pj = Cpr_pj·(d_p,x·cos θ_j − d_p,y·sin θ_j), with Cpr_pj = Σ_b m_b·P_bp·L_bj
- M_jk = Crr_jk·cos(θ_j − θ_k) (+ Σ I on the diagonal), with Crr_jk = Σ_b m_b·L_bj·L_bk
- Q_p = F_p − b_p·ṡ_p − g·Σ_b m_b·P_bp·d_p,y + Σ_j Cpr_pj·(d_p,x·sin θ_j + d_p,y·cos θ_j)·ω_j²
- Q_j = Gr_j·sin θ_j − Σ_k Crr_jk·sin(θ_j − θ_k)·ω_k² + τ_j − c_j·(ω_j − ω_base), minus the torque τ_i − c_i·(ω_i − ω_j) of each hinge i mounted on the part hinge j turns (the reaction); Gr_j = g·Σ_b m_b·L_bj

M(q)·q̈ = Q is solved by LDLᵀ without pivoting, as the chain block does today. M is positive definite because every joint must move mass (refused otherwise).

- **One state node per mechanism**: an `ode` node with `states`, `init`, `out` (taps: each joint's coordinate and rate), `make` (allocation-free; several inputs through a `uv` Float64Array) and `bound`. Model mode, structural reduction, `linearize.js` and the planner see an ordinary physics node and do not change.
- **States**: the rails' coordinates, then the hinges' angles, each in block order, then their rates in the same order. Labels `subsystem/coordinate` (`cart and 3 rods/θ1`); units m, rad (angle flag), m/s, rad/s.
- **bound** (the fastest dynamics, for the step size), from the structure: 4·√(g / l_min·(1 + hinges)) + Σ b / M_pp + Σ c / M_jj, a uniform rod counting 2l/3. Cart–pole 25.1 (the block said 9.8), triple 50.2 (unchanged). Neither changes a step: in the simulation the record's Ts and the Controller's sample time bind, and the planner's 5 ms cap keeps 2 substeps per 10 ms.
- **Refusals**, each a sentence naming the parts: a closed loop ("a mechanism must be a tree"); a part not joined to the World; two Worlds joined; a part joined the wrong way round ("its base (B) goes on the World's side"); a rail on a part that turns; a joint that moves no mass ("put a Mass on it"); two joints with the same coordinate name.
- `pose(q)` gives every frame's position (for tests and a later view); `describe()` the equations with numbers; `sheet()` the equations in names (§1.6).

### 1.2 Equations (the Function block, generalised)

Type `fcn` stays (files keep loading); its label becomes **Equations**.

- Parameters: `inputs` (names, default `u`), `eqs` (lines `name = formula`, default `y = u^2`), `outputs` (names, default `y`). One port per input and per output, each labelled with its name.
- A formula may use the block's inputs, names defined on earlier lines, the parameters of the masks around it (folded to constants once per compile), `t`, `pi`, `e` and expr.js's functions. Names are case-sensitive and may be Greek.
- **An unknown name is refused** with a sentence listing what is there ("'l4' is not an input, a name defined above, or a parameter (Q, h, r0, R, H, a, g, h0)"). Today's Function reads NaN silently. Also refused: a name defined twice, an output that is not computed.
- It is a pass-through block: a loop through Equations without a state is an algebraic loop, as today. In model mode it is evaluated as in the simulation, so finite differences see its slopes.
- **Migration**: `{ expr }` loads as `{ inputs: 'u', eqs: 'y = <expr>', outputs: 'y' }`. The compiler performs the same floating-point operations as expr.js (it folds only subtrees that are constant, which give the same numbers), so old Function blocks run bit for bit (a test holds the library's four Function blocks to that).
- Compiler: `core/formula.js` (145 lines in the prototype): parse to a tree, fold constants, build closures over a Float64Array of slots; no `eval`, no `new Function`. expr.js stays for signals and views (merging the two is later work).
- It is A's general fallback for force laws, motor models and the PUMA's coordinate transformations (task #24).

### 1.3 Integrator with a start value, limits, unit and angle

`integrator` gains `x0` (0), `min` and `max` (empty: none), `unit` (`1`) and `angle` (0/1). Number fields may be formulas in the mask's names (`x0: "h0"`, `max: "H"`).

- Rate K·u, except 0 where the state is at min and K·u < 0, or at max and K·u > 0. Output: the state clamped to [min, max]. The start value is clamped into [min, max]. This is Simulink's limited integrator, and it is exactly main's tank overflow and its dry bottom.
- Its state is labelled with the block's name (`tank/h`), with the unit and the angle flag, so the design layer and the planner read it as they read a physics state.
- With the fields at their defaults it runs as today, bit for bit (dX = K·u, y = x). `min > max` is refused.
- `bound` 0, as today: a diagram of primitives steps at its record's Ts (§4, the tank).

### 1.4 Subsystem masks and formula parameters

- A subsystem may carry `mask: [{ key, label, unit }]` and `params: { key: number | formula }`.
- Inside it, any number parameter may be a formula in the names of the masks around it, the innermost first (`"l1"`, `"2*m"`). A mask value may itself be a formula in an outer mask.
- Resolved at compile time, outermost first, to numbers. Equations blocks read the names as constants.
- Refusals: an unknown name ("'l4' is not a parameter of 'cart and 3 rods' (M, m1, …)"); a key that is `t`, `pi`, `e` or a function name; a key twice; a formula in an integer, name or choice field.
- **Selected, a masked subsystem shows its mask fields** in the Parameters panel (typed, SI prefixes, the wheel), with Open. Inside, a field holding a formula shows it with its value ("l1 → 0.25 m").
- `plantKey` includes the mask values, so editing one re-designs and moving the block does not (as for any parameter).
- **The API as built** (2026-10-25; scalar; vectors are robot.md's):
  - Declare: `{ "type": "subsystem", "mask": [{ "key": "G", "label": "Gear ratio", "unit": "1", "min": 1, "max": 500, "default": 100 }], "params": { "G": 107.8 }, "diagram": … }`. `key` is letters, digits, _ (Greek too), not `t`, `pi`, `e`, `inf` or a function; `unit` defaults to `1`; `min`, `max`, `default` are optional numbers. A value may be a formula in the masks outside (not its siblings) and may be `inf`.
  - Read: inside, any number field (`ELEMENTS` params without a `kind`; a model block's numbers; each `num`/`den` entry) may be a formula string in the masks' names, innermost first; an Equations line reads them as constants. Integer fields refuse formulas. Saturation and Integrator limits may be `±inf`; other fields must come out finite.
  - Code: `normalizeDiagram(d, path, { scope, resolve })` checks and keeps formulas as written; `compileDiagram` resolves them (`resolve: true`) before compiling; `maskValues(diagram, idPath)` gives the names' values at a level (a Map, innermost first) and `formulaIn(src, values)` a field's value (the Plant tab's panel uses both); `core/formula.js` has `compileFormula`, `evalFormula`, `isFormula`, `isReserved`, `sentence`.

### 1.5 Compiler changes every part needs

1. **Pass nodes are elided.** In, Out, Sink and Terminator nodes only forward a value: inputs read their origin, and they are not evaluated; probes read through. The subsystem wrapper then costs nothing per step, and today's blocks get faster too (the triple's eval 0.97–1.0 µs → 0.87–0.93 µs). Values are unchanged bit for bit.
2. **State nodes with several inputs** (`uv`), for a mechanism with force and torque inputs.
3. **`blockInputNames` reads a subsystem's Out name** (one line), so a Controller's inputs read x, θ1, … and not "cart and 3 rods".
4. `el.mechanisms` lists the compiled mechanisms (for the sheet and, later, a view).

### 1.6 The equations sheet (from B)

`core/sheet.js` (137 lines in the prototype) walks the same tree as `mech.js`, with polynomials in the parameters' text instead of numbers, and factors common terms out: l1·l2·(m2 + m3). It renders:

- the coordinates ("x (rail), rate v; θ1 (hinge, from upright), rate ω1; …");
- one row per coordinate, Σ_b M_ab·q̈_b = Q_a, with the derived velocity terms;
- V(q) and T = ½·q̇ᵀ·M(q)·q̇.

The cart–pole reads:

```
(M + m)·v̇ + l·m·cos θ·ω̇ = F − b·v + l·m·sin θ·ω²
l·m·cos θ·v̇ + l²·m·ω̇ = g·l·m·sin θ
V = g·l·m·cos θ
```

A parameter typed as a number shows as that number. Inputs are named by what is wired into them (F, from the In port), not u1. The production version handles inclined rails (cos α and sin α as factors); the prototype covered horizontal rails only. `check(scope)` evaluates every coefficient, so a test holds the sheet to the matrices the simulation uses.

### 1.7 On the canvas (not prototyped)

- **Frame ports** are small squares on a part's bottom edge (B on the left; F or T on the right; W and a Mass's F in the middle). Signal ports stay on the left and right edges.
- **Joins** are thick grey lines without arrowheads. A join of three ports has a dot where they meet. Drag from a frame port to another, or onto a join's dot, to join. Select a join and press Del to remove it. Deleting a part removes its ports from its joins.
- **Mechanism errors** show as algebraic loops do: a red sentence, the parts outlined.
- **Group** (Ctrl+G) refuses a selection that holds part of a mechanism: "rail and cart are joined to hinge 1, which is not selected: a mechanism stays in one diagram (select all of it, or none of it)".
- **Library.** A new group, Mechanics: World, Rail, Hinge, Mass, Rod. The Physics group becomes templates: Cart–pole, Double pendulum on cart, Triple pendulum on cart, Conical tank, each inserting the masked subsystem. Math's Function becomes Equations; Dynamics' Integrator gains its fields.
- **Faces**: the parts' laws (§1.1); Equations shows its first lines (all of them in the tooltip); the Integrator "∫" with "[0, H]" when limited.
- **The sheet** is a fold, "Equations of motion", in the Parameters panel of any part of a mechanism (open on the World), and of a masked subsystem that holds one mechanism, so it can be read without opening.

| | Today (block) | Open subsystem |

- **Unchanged**: model mode, `el.seen`, structural reduction (carts 4 or 8 states, tank 1), `linearize`, `trim`, `firstOrderFit`, every designed type (`lqr`, `swingUpLqr`, `scheduledPI`, `trajectory`, `kalman`), the planner, `verifyLoop`, snapshots, `linearizeBlock`, `loopModel`. The design statuses read "F → x, θ, v, ω · 4 states (x, θ, v, ω)".
- **Changes**: the cart–pole's state order is the chain's (positions, then rates); nothing stored depends on it (Q, K and the swing-up's inputs are in measurement order), but six tests assert the old order (§8.6), and a Kalman observer on a cart–pole now estimates (x, θ, v, ω). Labels use `/` as for any block in a subsystem.
- **A merge item, settled** (found while measuring): main's tank overflows at H = 1 m and feat/designs scheduled a PI at 1 m. At h = H the level's rate has no slope above, and the design fails (on the merged branch: "pole cancellation needs slopes, and the plant has a kink at h = 1 m"). The merge moved the top level to 0.95 m (a design input; decisions v2.7.2), and all three variants design.

## 6. Migration

**Old projects.** `normalizeDiagram` replaces a `cartPole`, `pendulumCart` or `conicalTank` block with its subsystem (`expandPhysics`).
- It keeps the id, name, position and flip. The old parameters, completed with the old defaults, become the mask values; `links` becomes the number of hinge and rod pairs.
- The port order is the same, so wires such as `cart:3`, the Controllers' measurement order and the Sinks still fit.
- There is no version bump (the format stays `loop-tuner/2`). A saved file holds the subsystem from then on.
- The old types, their `ode` definitions and `chainRates` leave ELEMENTS (about 105 lines). A 15-line table of their defaults stays in `physics.js` for the migration. The `ode` node kind stays (mechanisms use it).

**The System library.** `plants.js` builds the three blocks with the generators, for example `{ id: 'cart', name: 'cart and 3 rods', x: 400, y: 60, ...pendulumOnCart(3, { M: 1, m1: 0.1, l1: 0.25, …, theta10: Math.PI, … }) }`. Nothing else in the entries changes (the tank's top schedule level is the merge's, §3).
- **Tags**: `systemTags` walks subsystems already. Its nonlinear set gains `hinge`, and an Integrator with a limit (a mechanism of rails alone is linear). Equations counts as Function does today.
- **Other plants** (motor, oven, encoder, sliding mode) are already built from primitives. The HIL plant may stay a black box (the person's rule).

**Project format** ([project-format.md](project-format.md)): a diagram may hold `joins`; a subsystem may hold `mask` and `params`; a number parameter may be a formula string; Function's parameters are `inputs`, `eqs`, `outputs` (an old `expr` is accepted); the Integrator has `x0`, `min`, `max`, `unit`, `angle`.

## 7. Views

Unchanged. Views bind to Sources and Sinks, which keep their names, and every library view draws the same state.

Later, not in this plan: a `mechanism` view shape drawn from the mechanism's own `pose(q)`, so the rod lengths (0.25 and 0.5, typed again in `cartView`) and the tank's slope are not typed twice and the picture cannot disagree with the model; or views that read mask values (`cart.l1`).

## 10. Not built (and why)

- **Frame ports on subsystem boundaries**, so a mechanism can span subsystems (a rod as a reusable sub-assembly). Needed for detail levels inside a mechanism and for reusable parts. Detail levels of a whole plant (task #25) work without them: the open cart is level 1, with the same ports as a later level with uniform rods or hinge friction.
- **Relative hinge angles** (a constant transform of the coordinates, so the fast form stays fast). The convention stays today's absolute angle.
- **Springs, end stops, contact, wheels, gears as parts.** A spring can be wired today through a joint's input (x → Gain −k → Sum → the rail's F). End stops need events.
- **Closed chains** (four-bar) **and rails on turning parts** (ball and beam) are refused. C's general solver, kept in the tests, handles them at about 2.6 times the cost; it can be admitted behind the same parts when an example needs it.
- **3D and the PUMA** (task #24): the parts map one to one onto URDF links and joints. A 3D articulated-body solver behind them is its own design, to be measured before it is promised.
- **Vessel and Orifice parts** (C's original tank): not needed; the tank is primitives.
- **A fused evaluator for pass-through slices** (A's interpreter). It would bring the open tank's evaluation from 0.57 µs back to about 0.15 µs. Build it when a plant of primitives becomes planner-critical.
- **Generated JavaScript (`new Function`)**: never. The page's security policy is expected to refuse it, and the project decided on no eval.
- **A computed step bound for diagrams of primitives** (the Jacobian's eigenvalues at the start state, B's idea). It would not restore the tank's old step: |λ| is about 13–17 at the start level (depending on the inflow) against the block's hand bound of 282 at 1 cm, and near empty the √h has no finite bound. The record's Ts is the knob if the person wants the old accuracy.
- **Editing masks in the page** (adding, renaming or removing a mask parameter): JSON and templates only for now. "Make this a parameter" on a field is the natural later gesture.
- **A Mechanics block with M(q), V and Q typed by hand** (B) and **visible integrators with a Linear-solve block** (A): rejected. In both, the physics the person reads is typed, and an edit can silently break conservation. A 6×6 M(q) for the PUMA cannot be typed. Both were slower than C without generated JavaScript (planner 1.2–1.7 times).
- **A mechanism view from `pose(q)`; views reading mask values** (§7).
- Merging expr.js into `formula.js` (two formula compilers until then).
