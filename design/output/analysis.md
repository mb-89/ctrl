# Tab 5, Analysis

Reader: whoever changes the Analysis tab or the loop analysis in `src/core/analysis.js`.

The person asked for the diagrams control engineers read before tuning: root locus, the Ortskurve (Nyquist), Bode with margins, stability diagrams, and an X–Y plot of the error over the reference ([input](../input/2026-10-24-09-analysis-autotune-triple-swingup.md)). Research, thresholds and sources: [../research/analysis.md](../research/analysis.md).

**Status**: built and tested; the 2026-10-24 review's findings, and what its verification found, are fixed. Its plant comes from the design layer (`app.loopModel`, merged from `feat/designs`, decisions v2.7.2): every controlled system with a linear law shows its numbers, and one without says why (a relay, the ideal sliding mode, super-twisting, a swing-up pumping energy, a plan not made yet, a plant outside the page).

## What it shows

- **Left, the key numbers**, each with a word and a mark (✓ good, ! fair, ✕ poor), never colour alone. Only a stable loop is graded: an unstable one has no margins to grade. **Nor is a loop where the linear model does not hold**: at a kink at the operating point (the slopes differ left and right, so the central one is neither side's: the empty tank) or with u at a limit there (the oven at rest), the verdict first says "! not linear here" and why, then "as linearised: stable" (or marginal, unstable), and no number is graded (`loopModel`'s `plant.kinks` and `atLimit`). Before, the empty tank at rest read "✓ stable", gain margin ×4840 "good" and a step that settles in 1240 s, with the kink only in the notes below.
  - the verdict: **stable** (every closed-loop pole inside the unit circle), **marginal** (none outside, but one on the circle: a free integrator or an undamped mode, which neither dies away nor grows), or **unstable** (how many are outside);
  - the gain interval: the factors by which the loop gain may change before a closed-loop pole crosses the unit circle, both sides for an open-loop unstable plant. For an unstable loop the row reads **Stable for** gain ×a … ×b instead: the gains at which every closed-loop pole would be strictly inside the circle (or "no gain"; gains where poles only sit on the circle do not count);
  - the phase margin at the crossover; the delay margin in ms and samples. An unstable loop has neither: its row reads **Phase at |L| = 1**, how far the phase is from −180° there, and the delay margin "—";
  - Ms = max |S| (good ≤ 1.4, fair ≤ 2) and Mt = max |T|;
  - the slowest pole's time constant (τ = ∞ on the circle; "grows" for an unstable pole) and the least damping ratio ζ;
  - Nyquist's P (open-loop poles outside), N (encirclements of −1) and Z = N + P; open-loop poles on the circle (integrators) are in neither P nor Z and are counted beside them;
  - the linear step's overshoot and settling time (2 %), both against its final value, and the final value itself: the closed loop's DC gain. A response still outside the band at the end of its window says "not settled in T s";
  - notes: the operating point, the variant, Ts, for Scope time the instant and where it came from, dead time in samples (fractions too), the model's warnings, and that the analysis is linear (u inside its limits, with the peak of u in the step per unit of r, so a feedforward kick far beyond the limits shows; the step goes through the prefilter, which is outside the loop).
- **Right, four plots** in the scope's dark style:
  - **Root locus**, s-plane (s = ln z / Ts) or z-plane: open-loop poles ×, closed-loop poles ●, and the closed-loop poles for the loop gain scaled ×0.01 … ×100. The s view shows where the loop works: poles up to ten times the crossover (or the slowest pole); faster ones are counted, not drawn, and so are poles at z = 0 (|z| < 10⁻³: a dead-time chain, a derivative's previous measurement), which s = ln z / Ts would put thousands of rad/s out. ζ = 0.7 rays; the unit circle in z.
  - **Bode** of the open loop L: magnitude and unwrapped phase, the crossings marked, PM and the GMs that bound the gain interval labelled (for an unstable loop "stable below ×b" / "stable above ×a" and "… from −180°" instead: no margins). A label at the ω = 0 end goes below the axis title. The axis runs from a decade below the loop's slowest pole to just below the Nyquist frequency π/Ts; a crossing at an end of the band (z = 1 or z = −1) is marked at that end.
  - **Nyquist** (Ortskurve) of L for positive frequencies (solid) and negative (dashed), the radius compressed above 1 (1 + ln |L|) so integrators fit while −1 stays put; the unit circle, −1, and the Ms circle (|1 + L| = 1/Ms). It opens on the region around −1.
  - **Step r → y** of the linear closed loop, to twice its settling time, with its final value dashed when that is not 1. The step of r goes through the controller's prefilter Tr (carried on its linear model, outside the loop) as the law's does, so a PID with a prefilter shows the response it really gives, not the P kick of an unfiltered step. A velocity or acceleration feedforward still kicks at a step (Kv/Ts, Ka/Ts² per unit of r without a prefilter: 170 V for the sine's Kv 1.7); the limits that would cut it are not in the linear model.
- **Linked**: hovering the Bode plot marks that frequency on the Nyquist curve and reads out |L| and ∠L (the unwrapped phase the curve shows). The wheel zooms the locus and Nyquist plot about the pointer, up to zoom in as on the scope; a double-click restores the automatic view.
- **Scope time** follows the scope: the state under the cursor (or where it last pointed), at the sweep while the scope runs, else at the right edge of the screen. While it follows, the tab recomputes at most four times a second, counted from the end of the last analysis, and waits twice as long as that one took: a slow loop (a 100-sample dead time takes about 0.3 s) uses at most a third of the page's time.
- **The X–Y plot** is the scope's XY mode with Δ ([instrument.md](instrument.md)), not a diagram here: it needs no linear model.

## Where the numbers come from

- **The loop** is cut at the Controller's output u: L(z) = −u_c / v with the plant input u_p = v and r = 0 (`openLoop`). Negative feedback: the loop is stable when 1 + L has no zero on or outside the unit circle.
- **The plant** is the plant the block sees, linearised at an operating point: at rest (origin), the start state, or the state at the scope's time. It comes from the design layer (`linearizeBlock`, [designs.md](designs.md)) through `app.loopModel(path, op)`, with the page's design store, so a plan is never made twice; the tab's cache holds the store's generation, so a plan that settles is drawn at once. A plant outside the page (an External plant) has no model: the tab says so.
- **Discrete, as simulated**: the plant is discretised with a zero-order hold at the controller's Ts (`discretePlant`), so the margins include the sampling delay. Dead time on a measurement is exact: whole samples are a chain of unit delays; a fraction f of a sample more is the modified z-transform (the measurement is the plant (1 − f)·Ts after a sample instant, under the input held since then, read one delay later). Checked against a continuous-time simulation of the sampled loop: the critical gains agree to four digits. Measurements the controller does not read get no chain.
- **The controller** is its law's own linear model, `linear(p, Ts, at)` in `controllers.js`: PID (integral, filtered derivative, reference feedforward in r's column), state feedback, the scheduled PI at the operating point's y, the boundary-layer sliding mode inside its layer, and the swing-up where it catches (it refuses where it pumps energy); a designed variant gives its Copy as numbers twin's, from the numbers its design computed (the planned swing-up: the plan's gains at the sample the scope's time is at, frozen, with a note). The relay, the ideal sliding mode and super-twisting have none. The prefilter is not in its matrices (it is outside the loop, so it changes no margin or pole); the model carries its time constant Tr for the step.
- **Frequency response** (`respondAt`, `frequencyData`): L is solved at each frequency from its parts, without the delay states: the plant (zI − Ad)⁻¹Bd, each measurement's reading of it, its chain of unit delays as exactly z^−len, and the controller from each measurement, L = −Σ_k K_k(z)·z^−len_k·G_k(z). Every path round the loop passes a measurement, so the shortest chain among the measurements read is a factor z^−d of the whole loop; it is taken out and its phase added back exactly (−ω·d·Ts), so long dead times unwrap right. The grid: 400 log-spaced points from 0.1 × the slowest pole's |s| (open or closed loop; integrators and z = 0 left out), lower while |L| still moves towards 1, at least two decades below π/Ts, refined where the phase moves more than 45° between points. Measurements read with different dead times leave a chain in the core (its longest, `spread`, samples), whose phase turns once every 2π/(spread·Ts) rad/s; a linear grid of 24 points to a turn is added, so every −180° crossing is found (0 and 100 samples: 46, as dense evaluation gives; before, 12).
- **Margins** (`loopMargins`): every gain crossing gives a phase margin and a delay margin ((∠L + 180°) mod 360° / ωc); every −180° crossing gives a gain factor. Both are solved exactly between grid points. L is real at the ends of the band, z = 1 (ω = 0) and z = −1 (ω = π/Ts): a negative value there is a crossing of −1 too (a first-order plant under P, an unstable pole under P, the pendulum's catch loop). Ms and Mt over the grid.
- **The gain interval** (`gainRange`): stability can change only at those crossing gains (and where 1 + k·D = 0). The stretches between them are checked on the closed-loop poles (the stretch the tuned gain is in at k = 1, the others in their middle), outwards from the tuned gain until stability changes; the root locus's gains catch a change the grid missed, which is then found by bisection. A loop with no pole outside keeps the stretch where none goes outside (a pole touching the circle is passed over); an unstable loop gets the nearest stretch where every pole is strictly inside. The root locus has 121 gains, fewer for a loop of more than 40 states (each is an eigenvalue problem of that size).
- **Poles**: the open loop's state matrix is block triangular (plant, delay chains, controller), so its poles are found block by block: a plant integrator and a controller integrator stay two simple poles at z = 1, and a delay chain sits exactly at 0. The closed-loop poles are the eigenvalues of its state matrix; the root locus is the same for k·L. A pole within 10⁻⁷ of |z| = 1 is on the circle (`poleSides`). A repeated pole of a defective matrix (a double or triple integrator in a rotated basis) comes out of eig as a small ring around its place, up to 9·10⁻⁷ across here, partly outside the circle; the ring's mean is accurate. Which close poles are such a ring is checked on the matrix, not guessed from their distance: where poles within 10⁻³ of the circle and of each other fall on different sides, the matrix is changed by 1024·eps of its largest entry (`eigPerturbed`, balanced, a fixed pattern) and its eigenvalues found again. A pole that moves by more than a quarter of the distance to its nearest neighbour is a piece of a ring; the pieces are graded together by their mean. Poles eig resolves hardly move and keep their own sides: s = ±0.03 at 1 ms (z = 1 ± 3·10⁻⁵) are one pole outside and one inside, and a PI integrator 10⁻⁵ from a slow plant pole stays on the circle. Before, a fixed 10⁻⁴ grouped those too and averaged them onto the circle.
- **The step** (`stepInfo`): the closed loop from r to y as one state space, the prefilter one more state (`closedLoopModel`); the final value from (I − A) x = B; the window is ten time constants of the slowest closed-loop pole, stepped sample by sample for the first 20 000 samples and then exactly in strides of 2^p samples (A^M and its input sum), so a slow loop at a fast Ts costs about the same. A stride follows a mode that changes little over it (decays by under 5 %, turns by under 0.2 rad); while a mode it would not follow is alive (until e^−20 of it is left), the run goes on sample by sample, within a budget of work. Overshoot and the 2 % band are judged on every sample run, and the stride in which the response last leaves the band is run again sample by sample, so the settling time is exact to the sample (before, up to one stride off). The plot keeps the extremes of each bucket of samples.
