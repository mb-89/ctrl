# System library

Reader: whoever adds an example system or changes how systems load.

**A system is a complete example: its input signals, its plant (diagram, with any Controller blocks and their variants) and its views. It loads whole or by part, so the input of one system can drive the plant of another.** (The person, 2026-10-24: "call them system library".)

**Few systems, each one working and moving, each 5 s long** (the person: "so it doesn't overwhelm"). Controlled systems grow in difficulty (the person asked for 2–3 with "increasing complexity of controllers", and a triple pendulum for the complex one):

- **Tags** (the person, 2026-10-24): each system shows coloured tags in the System library: linear or nonlinear, dead time, its controller and observer types, a disturbance, frequency views, HIL, auto-tune. They are derived from the system itself (`systemTags` in `src/core/systems.js`); an entry may add its own. A system with an External plant states whether it is linear (a tag of kind `plant` in its entry), since its physics is outside the page. Values that differ look different at a glance (the person): the style says what a tag is about (plant: a filled chip; controller: a coloured bar; observer: an outline; feature: a dot; *No controller*: an empty dashed chip) and the tone which value it is, the same on every card. Linear and Nonlinear take the cool and warm poles (blue, red). No tag shares its tone with a tag beside it on a card, and no two values of one kind share one in the filter row: *Disturbance* is violet (it was Nonlinear's red on three cards), so *XY* is yellow, and *LQR*, beside Disturbance on both pendulums, is orange (State feedback, on no card with a disturbance now, took violet); *Dead time* is magenta (it was Auto-tune's green on the oven). *Disturbance* shows only where d plays something (a Source d whose signal has components; *Following a sine* keeps an empty d to fill). Tones come from an eight-slot palette validated for colour-vision deficiency on both themes' panels; the label is always shown, and a key under the popover's heading names the five styles. **Filtering** (the person): under the key, every tag in the library is a chip; a click on one (there or on a card) toggles it as a filter, and the list keeps the systems that carry all chosen tags (interpretation: several tags narrow), with a count and Clear (`libraryTags`, `filterByTags` in `systems.js`).
- **Physics is never wrong** (the person: "the control loops can be stupid, but the physics can't be wrong"). A bad controller may do strange things, but only what the real system would do: `test/physics.test.mjs` holds each physics block to conservation laws (energy, horizontal momentum, water volume) and textbook results (the hanging pole's frequency, a pole trailing at atan(a/g) under constant push), and the pictures draw the state as it is, without clamping. The cart runs on an endless rail, as in the model, so a runaway cart leaves the picture; the tank overflows at its height.
- **Following a sine (XY)** (XY demo): the DC motor follows a slow sine (0.2 Hz, one period per sweep); the scope opens in XY with Δ, error over reference. P: a wide ellipse (the error leads r by a quarter period); PD: a smaller one; PID: a thin tilted line (the integral turns the error against r); PID + feedforward (the same PID with a velocity feedforward Kv = 1.7 V·s/rad, set by hand) lays it flat on the axis once the start-up has died away (σ 0.0005 rad against the PID's 0.009 past 1.5 s). Pressing Time shows the same runs over time, the angle fitted again to its own scale. Its d plays nothing until the person gives it components. The motor diagram and picture are shared with *DC motor, position control* (`motorDiagram`, `motorViews` in `plants.js`).
- **The motor is no black box** (the person, input 10): Motor is a Sum, the Transfer function `armature 1/(Ls + R)` (den [L, R] = [0.01, 1]; its output, the current, is the Sink **i**), the gains `Kt (= Ke)` and `Ke (= Kt)` (0.5), and the Transfer function `rotor 1/(Js + b)` (den [J, b] = [0.02, 0.1]); each physics value appears once. Every motor system shares it (`motorDiagram(variants, { inputs, Ts })`); `inputs: 3` also feeds the speed to the controller and adds the Sink **e** = r − angle.
- **DC motor, sliding mode** (research: [sliding-mode.md](../research/sliding-mode.md)): three variants, each showing one thing. The relay (λ 10, K 12 V, φ 0) holds the shaft almost perfectly and pays with ±12 V at 62.5 Hz, 10 A of current ripple and 7.6 W of heat while holding still (1.0–1.49 s; in the notes' λ window, 0.4–1.4 s, the Measure lines read 126 reversals/s, 11 A p-p and 2.7 A rms, 7.3 W); the boundary layer (φ 2 rad/s) is a stiff PD inside (Kp 60, Kd 6): no chatter, but 33 mrad of error under the load; super-twisting (k1 4, k2 44) gives a continuous voltage and rejects the load to 0.34 mrad, keeping a 0.64 V ripple at 36 Hz from the armature. The controller runs at Ts = 1 ms; the entry records at 0.25 ms (`sim: { sampleTime: 0.00025 }`). The notes explain how to read λ (100 ms/div at 0.4 s, 0.1 rad/div, 1 rad/s/div: the line rises one division per division across) and the try-this edits (rotor den → [0.06, 0.1], J×3: the relay and the layer keep the slope, super-twisting first overshoots the line; armature den → [0.001, 1]: the relay chatters at 250 Hz with about five times the heat, 35.7 W while holding at the 0.25 ms record, 34.6 W exact). Measured against the research's table in `test/smc.test.mjs`.
- **Encoder errors and torque ripple** (FFT and spectrogram demo): a speed-controlled motor sweeping to 300 rad/s, an encoder with 1× and 2× angle errors, 6× torque ripple and a little torque noise; the encoder sits behind a flexible shaft that resonates at 70 Hz (the person asked for a resonance, to see what happens when the sweep runs through it). It opens in Spectrogram mode on the measured speed: each order is a line from the origin, the resonance a horizontal line, and each order flares up as it crosses it (6× at about 73 rad/s, 2× at about 220 rad/s). Switching the colour to Above noise shows the whole 6× line and its fold-back at the Nyquist frequency, which the absolute levels leave black.
- **Signals are named for what they are** (F in N, x in m, θ in rad, …). Controlled systems have a reference **r** and a disturbance **d**, so both following and rejection can be tuned (the person).
- **The page opens the Mass–spring–damper**, running, on its view. **Loading a system shows it running**: a whole load takes the title, scope channels and `ui` (run, Plant tab, its view, setup area at 42 %; the badly tuned oven opens on the Auto-tune tab instead).
- **Header, "System library ▾"**: every system, with Load and one button per part. **Plant tab, "System library" group**: a click loads the plant only.
- Entries live in `src/core/plants.js`; v1's closed-loop examples stay in `src/core/examples.js` for the CLI.

## What each design computes in the page

The library holds design **inputs** only: physics, hand gains, and the weights, levels and limits of designed variants. Every gain, table and start value a designed variant runs on is computed in the page from the plant as it is wired, every time the diagram compiles (cached in memory), so changing a mass or a radius changes the gains with it ([designs.md](designs.md); the person, [input 09](../input/2026-10-24-09-analysis-autotune-triple-swingup.md)). The Controller tab shows what was computed, in the **Computed** box under the parameters; **Copy as numbers** turns it into a hand variant.

- **DC motor**: hand gains, no design. P with Kp = 30 V/rad against 12 V limits rings; PD (Kp 20, Kd 1.5) is damped near ζ = 0.65; the PID adds Ki 10 and a 0.1 s prefilter. Its Motor is built from Transfer function blocks (above), so the design layer sees the current i and the speed as its states.
- **Encoder example**: a hand PI.
- **Conical tank** (`scheduledPI`; inputs: levels `at`, closed-loop time constant Tcl = 0.25 s, limits 0…3 m³/s): at each level the page trims the tank (the pump flow that holds it), linearises it and fits a first-order lag K/(τs + 1); pole cancellation gives Kp = τ/(K·Tcl), Ki = 1/(K·Tcl). For this tank that is Kp = A(h)/Tcl and Ki = a√(2g)/(2√h·Tcl): Kp 0.113 … 2.87 and Ki 2.80 … 0.909 over 0.1 … 0.95 m. The top level is 0.95 m, not the brim: the tank overflows at its height H = 1 m (the physics, main), so at 1 m the level's rate has a slope below and none above, a kink the design refuses ("pole cancellation needs slopes, and the plant has a kink at h = 1 m"; decisions.md, v2.7.2). The fixed PIs are the same design at one level (0.8 m: Kp 2.112, Ki 0.990; 0.15 m: Kp 0.173, Ki 2.287). u0, the flow that holds the start level 0.15 m, is a√(2g·0.15) = 0.1716, so every PI starts in exact balance. The pipe's 0.1 s dead time is reported and left out of the design; it is what makes the PI tuned for full ring when the level is low.
- **Pendulum** (`swingUpLqr` and `lqr`; inputs: Q = [10, 50, 1, 1] for x, θ, v, ω, R = 0.1, limits ±25 N): discrete LQR (at the block's Ts, 10 ms) on the cart and pole linearised at the upright rest gives K ≈ [−9.18, −63.2, −10.3, −13.3] and kr = K[0], so x follows r. The swing-up's energy pump keeps its hand gains (kE 3, kx 8, kv 5, catch below 0.4 rad), found by simulation: up in about 2 s, cart within ±1.2 m; the pole length and g it measures energy with (0.5 m, 9.81) are computed from the cart and pole too, so a longer pole is pumped to its own top.
- **Triple pendulum** (`trajectory` and `lqr`; Q = [10, 100, 100, 100, 1, 1, 1, 1], R = 0.1, limits ±40 N; the plan: T = 2.4 s, 12 segments, 75 % of the limit): when the system loads the page plans the swing-up from the cart and rods as they are wired (`planner.js`, about 3 s in Node, 5 to 7 s in a browser, during which the display draws at 20 frames a second): a force held per 10 ms sample that takes the chain from hanging to rest upright with the cart back at 0, peak about 15 N of the 30 N it may use. Along it, time-varying LQR (the Riccati recursion backwards from the upright LQR) corrects small errors; after it, the upright LQR (the same gains as "LQR only") holds the rods and takes the push. A check on the nonlinear plant confirms the rods are up 1 s after the plan before it is used; a plan that fails still runs, with a warning, so the attempt can be seen. Nothing of the plan is stored: changing a mass plans again.
- **Oven**: heater (lag 0.1 s), chamber (1 °C per %, lag 0.5 s), 0.1 s dead time to the sensor, thermocouple (lag 0.05 s); heater 0–100 %, Ts 20 ms. Its PID (Kp 6, Ki 12, Kd 0.1) is hand-picked to be poor (42 % overshoot); the Auto-tune tab computes better gains from the plant, nothing is stored ([autotune.md](autotune.md)).
- **Sliding-mode motor**: λ = 10 1/s (the error's time constant 0.1 s; sliding holds for |e| ≤ 2.4 rad even with J×3), K = 12 V (the limit), φ = 2 rad/s (the layer's speed loop β·K/φ = 150 1/s near the armature's corner 100 1/s, ζ ≈ 0.41; thinner layers ring), super-twisting k1 = 4, k2 = 44 (hand gains, [controller-proposal.md](controller-proposal.md)). Design inputs and hand gains only; nothing computed from the plant is stored.
- **HIL motor**: gains by hand against the script's physics (R 2 Ω, L 4 mH, k 0.1 N·m/A, J 2.5·10⁻³ kg·m², unbalance m g l 0.1 N·m, Coulomb friction 0.01 N·m): P with Kp 2 is slow and holding the arm up needs 2 V that it only gets from an error; PD (Kp 6, Kd 0.6) is fast with a smaller offset; the PID (Kp 10, Ki 20, Kd 0.6, Tr 0.1 s) settles on r. `test/live.test.mjs` checks that the script describes exactly the ports the system's External plant block has. It opens with the setup area at 36 %, since Live adds a row to Run Control. A variant from the plant has nothing to design on there: the page has no model of a plant outside it.
- Each design is checked against the closed-form values and the gains the library used to hold in `test/design.test.mjs`; the behaviour of every system in `test/core.test.mjs` (the oven in `test/autotune.test.mjs`, the sliding-mode motor in `test/smc.test.mjs`). A guard test refuses stored gain tables, start outputs and pipe start flows in the library; it allows the hand types `pid`, `off`, `slidingMode` and `superTwisting` beside the designed ones.

To add a system: add it to `plants.js` (name, notes, `signals`, `scope`, `ui`, `diagram`, `views`, and `sim: { sampleTime }` when it needs its own record step); it must move visibly with its own input within 5 s, and a test checks its behaviour. A scope channel may be assigned but off, or carry a Measure line: `channels('e', 'speed', { source: 'V', on: false, measure: true })`. Leave the scales to Auto Scale, which runs on load.
